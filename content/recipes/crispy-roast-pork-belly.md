## Ingredients

- 1 kg pork belly, skin on, in one piece
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 2 tsp salt
- 2 tsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp five-spice powder
- 1 tsp white pepper
- 1 tbsp white vinegar

## Method

1. Buy a piece of even thickness with the skin on and the ribs taken out, in one rectangle. A thin end cooks through before the thick end is done, and no oven fixes that.
2. Bring 3 litres of water to a rolling boil in a wide pan. Lower the belly in skin-side down and boil for 5 minutes, until the skin has tightened, turned opaque and pulled back a little at the edges. Lift it out and dry it all over.
3. Prick the skin with a metal skewer or a meat pricker, held straight down, several hundred times, until the whole surface is stippled. Through the skin only, never into the fat beneath. Every hole is somewhere steam gets out and a bubble forms, and the difference between a pricked skin and an unpricked one is the difference between crackling and leather.
4. Turn it over. Rub the shaoxing wine into the meat, then mix the salt, sugar, five-spice and white pepper and rub that into the meat and the four cut sides. Nothing goes on the skin: sugar burns, and salt pulls water to the surface, which is the opposite of what the skin needs.
5. Set it skin-side up on a rack over a tray. Pat the skin dry again and brush it with the vinegar. Refrigerate uncovered for 12 hours, and up to 24. The skin should come out dry to the touch, matt, and faintly leathery.
6. Heat the oven to 160 °C. Stand the belly on a rack over a tray and build a wall of foil around the four sides, up to the height of the skin, so that only the skin is exposed. Pack a 5 mm layer of coarse salt over the skin, edge to edge — about 300 g. Roast for 75 minutes.
7. Take it out and raise the oven to 240 °C. The salt will have set into a crust: lift it off in sheets, brush away every last grain, and pat the skin dry. Salt left behind burns black in the next stage.
8. Roast for 25 minutes, turning the tray once. It is done when the skin has blistered all over into small hard bubbles and gone the colour of dark honey. Pale patches will not crisp later: give each 30 seconds with a blowtorch, or turn the tray so they face the back of the oven for the last 5 minutes.
9. Rest for 20 minutes on a board, skin-side up and uncovered. Never under foil — trapped steam softens in five minutes what took twelve hours to dry.
10. Chop into 2 cm pieces with a heavy cleaver, skin-side up, cutting straight down through the crackling in one stroke. Sawing shatters it. Serve with mustard and sugar, or with hoisin.

## Notes

The 12 hours in the fridge is not optional and cannot be hurried. Everything
else here is recoverable; a skin that went into the oven damp is not.

The salt crust is armour rather than seasoning. It holds the skin flat, stops it
colouring before the meat is cooked, and draws the last of the water out of it.
It is cracked off and thrown away, which is why it is named in step 6 and left
out of the ingredient list — the same reason blanching salt is left out of the
two steamed patties. Counting 300 g of salt nobody eats would put 120 g of
sodium into the panel.

Five-spice belongs on the meat and only on the meat. Rubbed over the skin it
gives black speckles and a bitter crackling.

If the skin blisters unevenly, the belly was not sitting level. Prop the low
corner on a crumpled ball of foil.

Mustard and sugar is how it is eaten as a dish. Over a plate of rice it wants a
soy dressing instead, which is what a shop pours over a siu yuk rice box: Lee
Kum Kee's seasoned soy sauce for seafood is the bottle for it, and Roast Meat Soy
Dressing is the two-minute answer when there is none in the house. Either goes on
the rice and never on the meat, because anything wet touching that crackling
undoes twelve hours of work.

The panel reads 874 kcal a serving, and that is the belly as it goes into
the oven. A great deal of its fat renders into the tray and is poured away, so
this is one of the few recipes here where the figures overstate what reaches the
plate rather than understating it. How much depends on how hard you roast it and
is not a number worth inventing.

## Storage

Three days in the fridge, uncovered on a rack if there is room. Wrapped, the
crackling goes soft overnight, and a box is worse than a plate.

Bring it back at 200 °C for 8 minutes, skin-side up, which re-crisps it almost
completely. Never a microwave: it turns the crackling to rubber and there is no
way back from that.

Cold, it is very good chopped into fried rice, where softened skin stops
mattering. It does not freeze — the skin comes back damp whatever you do.
