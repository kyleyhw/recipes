## Ingredients

- 300 g chicken thighs, boneless and skinless
- 20 g ginger (a thumb), sliced
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 600 ml water
- 300 g cooked rice (2 bowls)
- 4 spring onions, chopped
- 20 g fried shallots (a large pinch each)
- 0.5 tsp salt
- 0.25 tsp black pepper

## Method

1. Put the ginger slices in a dry pot over medium-high heat for 60 seconds, until they smell sharp.
2. Add the chicken and sear for 3 minutes a side, without moving it, until both faces are golden and release from the pot on their own.
3. Pour the wine and soy sauce straight onto the hot base. Let them bubble for 10 seconds, scraping up everything stuck to the bottom — that is where the colour of the broth comes from.
4. Add the water, bring to a boil, then drop to a simmer for 15 minutes.
5. Lift the chicken out onto a plate and shred it with two forks. Return it to the pot.
6. Taste the broth. Add the salt only if it needs it; the soy has already salted it once.
7. Divide the rice between two bowls and pour the boiling broth and chicken over it. Leave it 60 seconds — the rice needs to take up some of the broth before it is worth eating.
8. Top heavily with the spring onions and fried shallots, grind over the pepper, and eat straight away.

## Notes

Cold, day-old rice is better than fresh here. Fresh rice collapses into the broth; cold grains keep their edges long enough to eat.

The fried shallots are not a garnish. Put on more than looks sensible — they are the only crisp thing in the bowl and they are gone within two minutes.

## Storage

The broth keeps three days in the fridge and freezes for two months. Store it apart from the rice, always: rice left in broth turns to porridge overnight.

To serve again, bring the broth back to a hard boil and pour it over fresh rice. Never reheat the assembled bowl.
