## Ingredients

- 100 ml water
- 50 g granulated sugar
- 100 g unsalted butter, softened
- 100 g caster sugar
- 0.25 tsp salt
- 2 eggs
- 100 g ground almonds
- 0.5 tsp almond extract
- 6 croissants, a day old
- 50 g flaked almonds
- 1 tbsp icing sugar

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 180 °C and line a baking tray.
2. Bring the water and granulated sugar to a boil in a small pan, stirring until the sugar has gone, then take it off the heat. It should be a thin syrup, not a thick one — 30 seconds at a boil is enough, and reducing it further gives something that sits on the surface instead of soaking in.
3. Beat the butter, caster sugar and salt with an electric whisk for 2 minutes until pale. Beat in the eggs one at a time, 30 seconds each; it will look split and does not matter.
4. Fold in the ground almonds and the almond extract with a spatula until smooth, 20 turns. You will have about 400 g of frangipane, which is 65 g a croissant.
5. Split each croissant horizontally, not quite all the way through, so it opens like a book and stays hinged.
6. Brush the syrup generously over both cut faces — about 2 tsp a croissant, and it should look like too much. The croissant is dry and this is the step that makes it moist rather than merely rich. A fresh croissant cannot take it, which is why this is made with yesterday's.
7. Spread 40 g of frangipane over the bottom half of each and close it. Spread the remaining 25 g over the top in a band down the middle, letting it come to the edges, and press the flaked almonds into it.
8. Bake for 18 minutes, until the frangipane on top has puffed, set and gone golden brown and the flaked almonds are toasted. The frangipane inside will still be soft.
9. Cool on a rack for 10 minutes, then dust with the icing sugar. Dusting them hot melts it into a wet glaze.

## Notes

This is a bakery's way of selling yesterday's croissants and it makes a better
pastry than the fresh one it started as. A day-old croissant is dry, which
means it drinks the syrup and holds its shape; a fresh one is already moist,
goes soggy, and slumps under the frangipane. Two days old is better than one.
If yours are fresh, split them and leave them out overnight.

Shop-bought croissants work perfectly well here and are what most people will
use — that is the honest reason this recipe and Croissants are two separate
files rather than one. Buy all-butter.

Frangipane is equal weights of butter, sugar, egg and ground almonds, near
enough, and two eggs at 50 g each is where the 100 g comes from. It is worth
knowing the ratio because it scales to anything.

Do not stint on the syrup. Undersyruped is the commonest fault and it is
invisible until you bite one, at which point the middle is dry and the whole
thing tastes of sweet almond paste. Two teaspoons a croissant, brushed into
both faces.

Almond extract is a quarter of what makes this taste of almond; ground almonds
by themselves are milder than people expect. Half a teaspoon in the whole batch
is the right amount and a teaspoon is too much.

A tablespoon of rum in the syrup is the traditional version and improves it.

## Storage

The day they are made, and preferably within four hours, while the top is still
crisp and the inside soft. They are the one thing here made from leftovers that
does not itself keep.

A day later the top has gone soft and the frangipane firm. Six minutes at
170 °C fixes both — better than it has any right to — but a second day after
that is too far.

The assembled, unbaked croissants keep overnight in the fridge covered, and
bake straight from cold for 20 minutes, which is the way to have them for
breakfast. The frangipane alone freezes for three months and thaws in the
fridge, so a batch made for six covers two more mornings.
