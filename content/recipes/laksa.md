## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 60 g laksa paste
- 400 ml chicken stock
- 400 g coconut milk (1 tin)
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 60 g tofu puffs (5), halved
- 200 g prawns (16), peeled
- 200 g dried rice noodles (2 portions)
- 100 g bean sprouts
- 2 eggs
- 1 lime, in wedges

## Method

1. Boil the eggs for 8 minutes, cool them under the tap, peel and halve them. Set aside.
2. Soak the rice noodles in a bowl of just-boiled water for 8 minutes, until bendy but still firm in the middle. Drain and set aside; they finish in the bowl.
3. Heat the oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the laksa paste and fry for 5 minutes, stirring most of the time, until it darkens by a shade and a distinct layer of red oil separates out around it. Malay cooks call this pecah minyak — the oil breaking — and it is the whole difference between a laksa and a bowl of coconut soup with spice in it.
4. Pour in the stock, bring to a simmer and cook 10 minutes.
5. Squeeze each tofu puff out under hot water like a sponge and drop them in. They are full of frying oil and will fill with broth instead. Simmer 3 minutes.
6. Turn the heat to low and add the coconut milk, the fish sauce and the sugar. Bring it to steaming and **do not let it boil** — coconut milk boiled hard separates into oil and grit, and it will not come back.
7. Add the prawns and cook 2 minutes, until they have curled and turned opaque all the way through.
8. Blanch the bean sprouts in a kettle of boiling water for 20 seconds in a sieve, then the noodles for 20 seconds to heat them.
9. Divide the noodles between two deep bowls, ladle the laksa over with the prawns and tofu puffs, and top with the bean sprouts and the halved eggs. Serve with lime wedges.

## Notes

**Fry the paste until the oil breaks.** Five minutes, medium heat, and you are
waiting for a visible red slick to separate around the paste rather than for a
smell. Everything else in this recipe is assembly; this is the cooking. A paste
that goes straight into liquid tastes raw and powdery and no amount of simmering
afterwards fixes it.

**Never boil it after the coconut milk.** The same rule as Miso Soup and for a
different reason: coconut milk is an emulsion, and a hard boil breaks it into
oil on top and chalky solids underneath. Low heat, steaming, and reheat
leftovers the same way.

Squeeze the tofu puffs. They come out of the bag full of the oil they were fried
in; run them under hot water and press each one flat, and they refill with
broth instead. This takes thirty seconds and is the difference between a puff
that tastes of laksa and one that tastes of old oil.

The jar is the ingredient. Prima Taste, Tean's Gourmet and the supermarkets' own
are all built the same way — chilli, lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, candlenut,
shallot and belacan — and nobody in a home kitchen pounds that from scratch on a
weeknight. Sixty grams is about four tablespoons and is a generous amount; some
jars are much hotter than others, so taste at step 6 before adding any sambal.

**Daun kesum is missing and there is no substitute for it.** Laksa leaf is what
a Malaysian bowl finishes with, it is not sold here, and mint and coriander are
sometimes suggested and taste like neither. It is left out rather than replaced,
and the bowl is good without it — if you can get it, a small handful shredded on
top at the end is the last five per cent.

Cockles and fish cake are the other two things in a hawker bowl and are both
left out for buyability. Fish balls are the easy addition if you want more in
it: they go in with the tofu puffs.

## Storage

The broth keeps three days in the fridge and it is worth making a double batch
of, since a jar of paste holds three or four pots' worth. Reheat it gently to
steaming — a boil after coconut milk splits it just as surely on day two.

It does not freeze well: coconut milk separates on thawing and the texture never
fully recovers. If you must, freeze it before the coconut milk goes in.

Assembled bowls keep for nothing. Noodles soaked and drained keep a day in the
fridge in a covered box.
