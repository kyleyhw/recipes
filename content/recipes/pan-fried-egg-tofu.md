## Ingredients

- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 2 tbsp cornflour
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 10 g garlic, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Snip one end off the plastic tube and slit it down its length with scissors, then let the tofu slide out onto a board in one piece. Do not try to squeeze it out — it tears.
2. Cut it into rounds 2 cm thick. You should get about ten.
3. Lay the rounds on a double layer of kitchen paper, put another layer on top, and press gently. Leave them 5 minutes. This is the step that decides the dish: egg tofu is mostly water and a wet round will steam in the oil instead of crisping, then break apart when you turn it.
4. Whisk the soy sauce, rice vinegar, sugar and sesame oil in a small bowl until the sugar has gone. Set it aside.
5. Put the cornflour on a plate and turn each round in it, pressing lightly so the flat faces and the curved edge are all coated, then tap off the loose flour. Do this just before frying; the coating goes gluey if it sits.
6. Heat the oil in a 24 cm non-stick frying pan over medium-high heat until a pinch of cornflour dropped in fizzes at once. Lay the rounds in with a slice, not touching.
7. Fry for 3 minutes without moving them at all. They are ready to turn when the edge you can see has gone from pale to deep gold and the round slides when you nudge the pan. Turn each one with a slice and a spoon, supporting it from both sides, and fry 3 minutes more.
8. Lift them onto kitchen paper for 30 seconds, then onto a warm plate in one layer — stacked, they steam each other soft.
9. Pour the dressing over, scatter the garlic and spring onion, and eat within a few minutes.

## Notes

Everything here is about water. Egg tofu is roughly 85% water held in a set
custard, and every problem people have with it — sticking, breaking, going
soggy — is that water. Press it dry, coat it dry, get the oil properly hot, and
then leave it alone for three minutes.

Leaving it alone is the hard part. The instinct is to shuffle the pan and it is
exactly wrong: the crust has to form before the round will release, and moving
it early tears the face off and leaves it stuck to the pan.

Cornflour, not plain flour. Cornflour goes glassy and brittle and stays crisp
under a wet dressing for a few minutes; wheat flour goes soft almost at once and
tastes of raw flour at this thickness.

The garlic goes on raw at the end. Fried in this much oil it would burn before
the tofu was done, and raw it gives the sharp edge that a dish of fried custard
needs. If raw garlic is not for you, fry it in a separate teaspoon of oil for 20
seconds while the tofu drains.

Do not use silken tofu here. It has none of the egg's setting power, and it
falls apart in the pan whatever you coat it in — that one wants Mapo Tofu, where
it is blanched and braised and never has to hold its own weight.

A non-stick pan is worth it for this and only this. Egg tofu will stick to
stainless steel however hot the oil, and once it sticks the dish is over.

## Storage

Eat it straight away. The crust is gone within ten minutes of the dressing
touching it, and that is the whole dish.

Fried plain and undressed, it keeps a day in the fridge and comes back
reasonably crisp at 200 °C for 6 minutes. Dressed, it does not come back at all.

Uncooked tofu keeps as the ingredient does: two days out of the tube, submerged
in cold water, in a covered box.
