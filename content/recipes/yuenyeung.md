## Ingredients

- 300 ml black tea, brewed very strong
- 150 ml filter coffee, brewed strong
- 120 ml evaporated milk
- 2 tsp granulated sugar

## Method

1. Brew the tea as for Hong Kong Milk Tea: 20 g of coarse Ceylon leaves boiled hard in 450 ml of water for 3 minutes, steeped covered off the heat for 5, strained through a cloth bag and pulled through it four times.
2. Brew the coffee strong — 20 g of medium-dark grounds to 200 ml of water in a filter or a cafetière, which is about half again the strength of a normal cup. Not espresso, which is four times as concentrated and will bury the tea.
3. Warm the evaporated milk in a small pan over low heat until it steams. Do not let it boil.
4. Combine the hot tea and hot coffee in a jug — seven parts tea to three of coffee — and stir once.
5. Put 60 ml of warm evaporated milk and a teaspoon of sugar into each cup, then pour 225 ml of the tea and coffee mixture into each. Stir once.
6. It is right when you can taste the coffee first and the tea last, with neither one winning. Drink it hot.

## Notes

Seven to three, tea to coffee, and it matters more than it sounds. Coffee is the
louder of the two even at half the volume, so an even split tastes like slightly
odd coffee and nothing else. Move it toward eight to two if your coffee is dark
roasted.

Both halves have to be brewed strong, because between them they are carrying
120 ml of evaporated milk. A normal cup of either, milked at this rate, comes
out grey.

Everything about the tea half of this — the strength, the cloth bag, the pull,
the evaporated milk — is Hong Kong Milk Tea, and that recipe explains why each
of them matters. This one only adds the coffee.

The name is 鴛鴦, the mandarin duck, which in Chinese painting is always a pair
whose male and female look nothing alike — a standing figure for two unlike
things that belong together. It is a good name for the drink and a better one
than anything English has managed.

Instant coffee is what a lot of cha chaan teng actually use, and it is not
absurd here in the way it would be in a cup of coffee: the tea and the milk
cover most of what is wrong with it. Filter is better. Espresso is worse than
either, because the crema and the concentration both fight the tea.

This is a drink that exists because it is caffeinated twice over. Two cups is
roughly a strong coffee plus a strong tea, which is the point of ordering it at
three in the afternoon and the reason not to at nine at night.

## Storage

Drink it straight away, like the milk tea it is half of.

Tea and coffee both keep two days in the fridge separately and neither is worth
reheating hot. Combined and poured over ice with the milk stirred in, they make
a good iced yuenyeung, which is the honest use for what is left in both jugs.
