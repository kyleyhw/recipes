## Ingredients

- 60 g tapioca pearls
- 500 g mango, flesh only
- 60 g granulated sugar
- 100 ml water
- 200 ml coconut milk
- 100 ml evaporated milk
- 230 g grapefruit (1 pink), segments only

## Method

1. Bring 1 litre of water to a rolling boil. Rain in the tapioca pearls, stirring at once so they cannot stick to each other or the base. Boil for 10 minutes, stirring every couple of minutes.
2. Take the pan off the heat, cover it, and leave it for 10 minutes. The pearls are ready when every one is clear all the way through with no white dot at the centre. If any still have one, cover and wait 5 minutes more.
3. Drain into a sieve and rinse under cold running water for 30 seconds, working them with your fingers until they stop feeling slippery. Leave them sitting in a bowl of cold water, or they will glue themselves into a block.
4. Peel two large mangoes and cut the flesh off the stones — 500 g of flesh. Blend four fifths of it to a completely smooth purée and cut the last fifth into 1 cm dice.
5. Heat the sugar and the water in a small pan, stirring until the sugar has dissolved and the syrup is clear. Take it off the heat and let it cool for 5 minutes.
6. Stir the syrup into the purée, then the coconut milk, then the evaporated milk. It should be the consistency of single cream and pour in a smooth stream. Cover and refrigerate for 2 hours, until properly cold.
7. Cut the top and bottom off the grapefruit, stand it up, and cut the peel and white pith away in strips following the curve. Cut each segment free from its membranes, then break the segments apart into loose strands with your fingers. Do this last: the strands go limp within an hour.
8. Drain the tapioca and stir it through the cold mango mixture. Ladle into four bowls and top with the diced mango and the grapefruit strands. Serve cold.

## Notes

Grapefruit stands in for pomelo, which is what the dish is named after and what
no ordinary supermarket stocks. Pomelo is drier, milder and coarser-grained, and
its strands stay separate and springy where grapefruit's soften and bleed a
little juice. Pink grapefruit is the substitution every home version makes and it
is a good one — it is more bitter than pomelo, which is not a problem in a
dessert this sweet. Use pomelo if you pass an Asian grocer.

The citrus is not a garnish. The whole dish is sweet mango and fat coconut, and
those strands of sharp bitter fruit are the only thing stopping it from being
cloying. Leaving them off gives you a nice mango drink and not this.

Tapioca pearls, sold as sago in most British supermarkets, and buy the small
ones — 2 mm. The large bubble-tea pearls take 40 minutes and stay chewy in a
cold dessert. The white dot in the middle is uncooked starch and it stays gritty
and chalky, so the resting step after the boil is doing real work; that is where
the last of it clears.

Rinse them and keep them wet. Tapioca that has drained dry in a sieve for ten
minutes becomes a single translucent lump that no amount of stirring separates.

Chill it properly before the tapioca goes in. Warm mango soup is unpleasant, and
the pearls firm up as they cool, which is what you want.

Coconut milk from a tin, the thick kind, not the cartoned drink sold with the
dairy milk — that is a tenth of the fat and gives a thin, watery bowl.

This is a good use for mango that has gone slightly too far. Overripe fruit
purées beautifully; it is only the underripe kind that cannot be rescued.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, covered, and it is genuinely good on the second day.

Keep the tapioca separate if you are making it ahead, in a bowl of cold water,
and stir it in when you serve. Left sitting in the mango overnight the pearls go
soft and swollen and give up their starch into it, which thickens the whole
thing into something pasty.

It does not freeze — the coconut milk splits and the tapioca goes to mush.
