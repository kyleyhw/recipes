## Ingredients

- 4 egg yolks
- 100 g caster sugar
- 500 g mascarpone, at room temperature
- 2 tbsp marsala
- 4 egg whites
- 300 ml espresso, cooled
- 200 g savoiardi (24 biscuits)
- 2 tbsp cocoa powder

## Method

1. Make the coffee first and leave it to cool to room temperature, 30 minutes. Hot coffee dissolves a savoiardo on contact, and warm coffee melts the layer of cream it sits under.
2. Whisk the yolks and sugar in a large bowl for 4 minutes, until the mixture has trebled in volume, turned pale straw, and falls from the whisk in a ribbon that sits on the surface for two seconds before sinking.
3. Add the mascarpone in three lots, folding each in with a spatula until smooth before the next goes in. Fold, do not beat: mascarpone splits into grains under a whisk and does not come back. Stir in the marsala.
4. Whisk the whites in a clean bowl to soft peaks — they should hold a shape that flops at the tip when the whisk is lifted. Stiff peaks fold in as lumps and stay as lumps.
5. Fold a third of the whites into the mascarpone to loosen it, then the rest in two lots, cutting down through the middle with the spatula and turning the bowl a quarter turn between strokes. Stop the moment no white streak is left.
6. Pour the coffee into a shallow bowl. Dip each savoiardo one second a side, counting it, and lay them in a single layer in a 20 × 25 cm dish. They go on absorbing after they leave the coffee — a biscuit that feels properly soaked in your hand will be sludge by morning.
7. Spread half the cream over them, right into the corners. Lay a second layer of dipped savoiardi across it at right angles to the first, and spread the rest of the cream on top. Smooth it flat.
8. Cover and refrigerate for 6 hours, and better overnight. This is not resting: it is the time the biscuit takes to draw moisture out of the cream and stop being a biscuit.
9. Dust the cocoa over the top through a fine sieve just before it goes to the table. Dusted early, it turns to dark paste on the surface.

## Notes

On raw eggs, plainly. The FSA changed its advice in 2017: British Lion-marked
eggs, which is nearly all UK retail eggs, are considered safe to eat raw,
including by pregnant women, infants and the elderly. It is not an absolute
guarantee, and several other food agencies still advise against raw egg in
tiramisu at all. That is the state of the information, and the decision is
yours.

If you would rather not, cook the yolks and leave the whites out. Whisk the
yolks and sugar in a bowl set over a pan of barely simmering water, not touching
it, for 6 to 8 minutes, until the mixture reaches 70 °C and holds a ribbon. Cool
it completely, fold in the mascarpone, and lighten it with 200 ml of double
cream whipped to soft peaks in place of the whites. Denser and more stable than
the classic, and it slices more cleanly.

Marsala is optional, and the Le Beccherie original in Treviso had no alcohol in
it whatsoever. Two tablespoons is enough to notice without tasting of drink.
Dark rum works; coffee liqueur makes it sweet.

Savoiardi, not sponge fingers from the biscuit aisle. Real ones are dry, crisp
and sugar-crusted, which is what lets them take up coffee without collapsing.
The soft supermarket sort turn to paste in the coffee bowl.

Real espresso or moka-pot coffee. Instant tastes of instant here, and there is
nothing in this dessert to hide behind.

One second a side is the single number in this recipe worth obeying to the
letter. Every failed tiramisu anyone has ever eaten failed in the coffee bowl.

## Storage

Three days in the fridge, covered. It is better on the second day than the
first, and after the third the bottom layer begins to weep.

It freezes for a month and does it remarkably well for something with no starch
in it: cover the dish tightly, defrost overnight in the fridge and never on the
counter, and dust the cocoa after defrosting rather than before.

Freezing suspends bacteria rather than removing them, so if you made it with raw
eggs, a month in the freezer buys keeping time and not safety.
