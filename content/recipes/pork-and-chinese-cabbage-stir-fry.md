## Ingredients

- 400 g fatty pork mince
- 1 tbsp ginger, minced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 40 g spring onions, chopped
- 400 g Chinese cabbage, roughly chopped
- 2 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 2 tbsp chilli oil
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 0.5 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.5 tsp msg
- 0.25 tsp white pepper

## Method

1. Cut the cabbage across into 3 cm pieces and put the thick white ribs in one pile and the leafy green tops in another. They cook at completely different rates and must go in separately.
2. Chop the spring onions and keep the white bases apart from the green tops, the same way.
3. Whisk the soy sauce, shaoxing wine, chilli oil, sesame oil, sugar, msg and white pepper together until the sugar and msg have dissolved. Mixing the chilli crisp into the liquid now is what stops its solids burning black in the wok later.
4. Put a wok over high heat for 90 seconds, until a drop of water skitters and vanishes. Add no oil — the pork brings its own.
5. Add the pork in one layer and leave it untouched for 30 seconds to take a sear, then break it apart with a spatula. Keep going for 5 minutes, until the fat has run out of it and the edges are golden and crisp. Do not drain that fat off; it is the frying medium for the next step.
6. Push the pork to one side of the wok. Drop the ginger, garlic and spring onion whites into the pool of fat in the empty half and fry them for 30 seconds, until they smell sweet and the garlic is pale gold. Then stir them through the pork.
7. Add the white cabbage ribs and toss for 2 minutes, until they have lost their squeak and turned slightly translucent at the edges.
8. Add the green leaves and toss for 1 to 2 minutes, until they have just collapsed and are still bright.
9. Pour the sauce around the rim of the wok, not into the middle — it should hiss and caramelise on the hot metal on its way down. Toss hard for 1 minute until everything is coated and glossy.
10. Turn off the heat, stir in the spring onion greens, and serve at once over hot rice.

## Notes

Ten per cent mince or fattier. Five per cent gives up almost no fat, and step 6 then has nothing to fry the aromatics in.

The ribs-then-leaves order is the whole technique. Put them in together and the ribs are raw when the leaves are gone.

Msg rather than more salt. The soy sauce and the chilli oil have already salted this to about 1300 mg of sodium a serving, and what 400 g of cabbage dilutes is savour rather than saltiness — msg is roughly a third the sodium of salt by weight and adds the thing that is actually missing. If you would rather not use it, half a teaspoon of salt does a different job and takes the dish to about 1900 mg.

More pork than the original, which ran about one part meat to two and a half parts cabbage. This is one to one, which makes it a main course rather than a vegetable dish with pork in it.

## Storage

Keeps three days in the fridge. The cabbage gives up water overnight, so pour off what has collected before reheating.

Reheat in a hot dry wok for 3 minutes rather than a microwave, which steams what is left of the crispness out of the pork.

Do not freeze. Cabbage thaws limp and watery, and the texture is most of the dish.
