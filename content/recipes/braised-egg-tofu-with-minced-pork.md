## Ingredients

- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 3 tbsp cornflour
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 150 g fatty pork mince
- 10 g garlic, finely chopped
- 10 g ginger, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 0.5 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 200 ml chicken stock
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Slit the tube down its length with scissors and slide the tofu out in one piece. Cut it into rounds 2 cm thick, lay them between two layers of kitchen paper, and press gently. Leave them 5 minutes.
2. Turn each round in 2 tablespoons of the cornflour, coating the faces and the edge, and tap off what is loose. Keep the last tablespoon back.
3. Heat the oil in a 24 cm non-stick frying pan over medium-high heat until a pinch of cornflour fizzes at once. Fry the rounds for 3 minutes a side without moving them, until both faces are deep gold. Lift them onto kitchen paper.
4. Pour off all but a tablespoon of the oil. Add the pork mince and break it up against the base. Fry for 4 minutes over medium-high heat, without stirring for the first 2, until it has stopped releasing water and the bottom layer has browned and caught slightly.
5. Add the garlic and ginger and fry for 30 seconds, until they smell sweet and have just turned pale gold.
6. Add the oyster sauce, soy sauce, shaoxing wine, sugar and white pepper, and let them bubble against the hot pan for 20 seconds — the wine should hiss and the smell should sharpen. Pour in the stock, bring to a boil, and simmer for 3 minutes.
7. Stir the last tablespoon of cornflour into 2 tablespoons of cold water until smooth, then pour it in in a thin stream, stirring. Simmer for 30 seconds more, until the gravy coats the back of a spoon and a line drawn through it on the pan base holds for a second before closing.
8. Slide the tofu back in, in one layer, and spoon the gravy over it. Simmer for 2 minutes without stirring — turning it now breaks it. It is done when the rounds have darkened to the colour of the sauce and a spoon pressed on one meets a soft, yielding middle.
9. Take the pan off the heat, trickle the sesame oil over, scatter the spring onion, and take it to the table in the pan. Serve with rice.

## Notes

The tofu is fried and then briefly braised, and the order matters more than it
looks. Fried alone it is crisp and bland; braised from raw it disintegrates into
the gravy. Two minutes at the end is long enough for it to take up the sauce and
short enough that the crust survives as a skin — which is the texture this dish
is actually about.

Two minutes and no stirring. Spoon the gravy over the rounds rather than moving
them through it. Egg tofu that has been fried is strong enough to lift and
nowhere near strong enough to stir.

Fatty mince, 20% if the butcher has it. The pork is browned in almost no oil and
has to render its own; 5% mince gives up nothing, steams grey, and leaves step 6
with no fat to fry the aromatics in.

Let the pork catch. The two minutes without stirring at the start of step 5 is
where the browning happens, and browning is most of what makes this taste of
more than soy sauce.

Two hundred millilitres of stock is a wet braise on purpose — this is a dish to
spoon over rice, and the gravy is half of it. If yours is thicker than double
cream after the slurry, add 50 ml of water.

Dried shiitake, soaked and chopped, go in with the aromatics and are the usual
addition. Minced prawn in place of half the pork is the other one.

## Storage

Three days in the fridge, covered, and it is one of the better things here on
the second day — the tofu keeps taking up the gravy.

Reheat in a pan over low heat for 5 minutes with a splash of water, or in the
microwave covered for 2 minutes. The fried crust is gone by then and what is
left is soft egg tofu in gravy, which is a good dish and not this one.

It does not freeze. Egg tofu thaws spongy and weeping, and the gravy will not
hide it.
