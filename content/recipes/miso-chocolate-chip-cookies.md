## Ingredients

- 170 g unsalted butter, softened
- 45 g miso
- 150 g brown sugar
- 75 g granulated sugar
- 1 egg
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 210 g all-purpose flour
- 0.75 tsp bicarbonate of soda
- 200 g dark chocolate, chopped into 1 cm chunks

## Method

1. Get the butter soft first — a finger pressed into it leaves a clean dent and the block does not slide away. An hour out of the fridge in a warm kitchen, three in a cold one.
2. Beat the butter and the miso together for 1 minute, until there is no paler streak of butter and no darker fleck of miso left. Doing this before the sugar goes in is the one structural change from the plain cookie: miso beaten into a sugared mixture stays in lumps, and a lump of miso in a finished cookie is a small brown patch of concentrated salt.
3. Add both sugars and beat for 3 minutes with an electric whisk, 5 with a wooden spoon. It goes from wet sand to the colour of milky coffee, and it is ready when a lifted beater carries a soft peak that folds over.
4. Beat in the egg for 30 seconds, then the vanilla for 10.
5. In a second bowl, stir the flour and bicarbonate of soda together for 10 seconds. There is no added salt in this recipe — the miso is carrying it — so there is nothing else to disperse.
6. Fold the dry into the wet with a spatula until the last streak of flour goes, no more than 15 turns. Fold in the chocolate with 5 more and stop while dry-looking seams remain.
7. Cover and refrigerate for 12 hours. This is not the same instruction as the hour in the plain cookie: miso is full of active enzymes and salt, and overnight they break some of the flour's starch down into sugar. The dough goes visibly darker and slacker, and the baked cookie tastes of caramel rather than of miso.
8. Heat the oven to 180 °C and line two baking trays.
9. Take the dough out and leave it 20 minutes, until a rolled ball does not crack at the seams. Divide into 12 and roll each into a ball of 75 g. Put 6 on each tray, 6 cm apart.
10. Bake for 12 minutes, swapping the shelves at 6 minutes. They brown faster and deeper than the plain version — the extra sugar from the rest sees to that — so start looking at 10. They are done when the outer 2 cm is set and dark gold and the middle is still pale, domed and wet.
11. Slide the paper onto a rack and leave them 10 minutes. They sink into ridges and the middles set to fudge.

## Notes

Forty-five grams of white miso replaces the full teaspoon of salt in the
Chocolate Chip Cookies, and it is not simply a salt substitution. It brings
roughly the same sodium, and with it glutamates and the products of two years
of fermentation, so the cookie reads as savoury in a way salt alone cannot
manage. Eaten blind, most people do not identify miso; they say the cookie
tastes more of butter.

White miso — shiro, the pale sweet one — and nothing darker. Red or hatcho miso
is three times as assertive and turns the dough grey and faintly medicinal.

Do not add salt on top of this. The miso is about 12% salt by weight, so 45 g
of it brings about a teaspoon's worth, which is exactly what has been taken
out. Flaky salt pressed onto the tops before baking is still worth doing.

The overnight rest is doing more work here than in the plain cookie, where it
is about firming butter and hydrating flour. Miso's enzymes are still alive in
a raw dough and they spend the night converting starch to sugar. Bake this
after an hour and it is a good cookie with a savoury edge; bake it after twelve
and it is a different and better one.

Everything else — the chopped bar rather than chips, 70% and not lower, the
6 cm spacing, pulling them while the middles look raw — is as it is in the
Chocolate Chip Cookies and for the same reasons.

## Storage

Four days in a tin at room temperature, and unlike the plain cookie these are
better on the second day than the first: the miso flavour, which can be sharp
straight out of the oven, settles into the butter overnight.

They go soft rather than hard. Four minutes at 160 °C followed by 5 minutes on
a rack crisps the edges again. Do not microwave them.

The rolled balls freeze on a tray in 2 hours and keep 3 months bagged — and
freezing after the 12-hour rest is the right order, because the rest is doing
chemistry that the freezer stops. Bake from frozen at 180 °C for 14 minutes.
