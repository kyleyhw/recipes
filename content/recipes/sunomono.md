## Ingredients

- 300 g cucumber (1 large)
- 0.5 tsp salt
- 5 g wakame
- 3 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp light soy sauce
- 5 g ginger, finely grated
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds

## Method

1. Put the wakame in a bowl of cold water. It rehydrates to about ten times its dry size in 5 minutes; drain it, squeeze it gently, and cut any long fronds into 3 cm pieces.
2. Slice the cucumber as thinly as you can — 2 mm, thinner than for a sandwich, thin enough that the slices are translucent at the edge. This is the one place a mandoline earns its keep.
3. Toss the slices with the salt in a bowl and leave for 10 minutes. They will go from stiff to floppy and a pool of water will collect underneath.
4. Take the cucumber up in handfuls and squeeze it gently but firmly, letting the water run away. Do not wring it — you want it limp and drained, not crushed. Three hundred grams comes down to about 200 g.
5. Stir the rice vinegar, sugar, soy sauce and ginger together until the sugar has dissolved. Taste: it should be sweet first and sharp second, which is what distinguishes sunomono from a vinaigrette.
6. Combine the cucumber, wakame and dressing. Serve cold, in small bowls, with the sesame seeds scattered over and a spoonful of the dressing in the bottom of each.

## Notes

This is a third cucumber salad in this collection and a third technique: salted
and squeezed rather than salted and drained, and sliced paper-thin rather than
smashed. The squeeze is what lets the dressing in. Drained-but-unsqueezed
cucumber has water in the cells that the vinegar cannot displace, and the salad
tastes of cucumber with vinegar next to it rather than of cucumber that has
become slightly pickled.

It is meant to be small and sharp. A sunomono portion is a few tablespoons in a
little bowl, eaten between mouthfuls of richer things — grilled fish, fried
chicken, a bowl of ramen. Serving it as a large side salad is not wrong but it
is not what it is for, and a whole bowlful is a lot of vinegar.

Seasoned rice vinegar — sushi-su — already contains the sugar and salt. If that
is what is in the cupboard, leave out the sugar entirely and taste before adding
the soy sauce.

Five grams of dried wakame is a scant tablespoon of flakes and becomes a
generous handful. Weighing it is worth the trouble the first time; after that
you will know the size of the heap.

## Storage

A day in the fridge, covered. It will get more sour and more limp, which is a
slow decline rather than a cliff — a sunomono on its second day is a pickle, and
a fine one.

Beyond that the wakame starts to break down and go slippery in a way that is not
pleasant.

It does not freeze.
