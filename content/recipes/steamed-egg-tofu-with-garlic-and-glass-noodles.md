## Ingredients

- 50 g glass noodles
- 30 g garlic, finely chopped
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 1.5 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 3 tbsp water
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Cut the bundle of glass noodles in half with scissors while it is still dry — once soaked it becomes a metre-long tangle you cannot divide. Cover with warm water and leave for 10 minutes, until they are translucent and pliable but still have a firm core. Drain them well.
2. Heat 2 tablespoons of the oil in a small pan over medium heat. Add two thirds of the garlic and fry for 90 seconds, stirring constantly, until it is an even pale gold. Tip it into a bowl at once, oil and all — it carries on cooking in the hot pan and goes bitter within seconds of turning brown.
3. Stir the remaining raw garlic into the same bowl, along with the soy sauce, sugar, water and sesame oil.
4. Spread the drained noodles in a loose nest over a heatproof plate with a rim.
5. Slit the tofu tube and slide the tofu out. Cut it into rounds 2 cm thick and lay them on the noodles, overlapping.
6. Spoon the garlic mixture over the tofu, letting some run down into the noodles.
7. Put a rack in a wok or wide pan, add water to just below it, bring to a boil, then drop to a steady simmer. Set the plate on the rack, cover, and steam 8 minutes. It is done when the noodles have gone fully clear and slippery and swollen with what has come out of the tofu, and the tofu is hot through.
8. Heat the last tablespoon of oil in a small pan over high heat until it smokes, pour it over the spring onion scattered on top, and serve at once.

## Notes

The noodles are the point of putting them there. Egg tofu sheds a lot of water
when it steams and every other version of this dish either pours it away or
lets it dilute the sauce — here it goes into 50 g of dried starch that wants
exactly that much liquid. Nothing is thrown away and the noodles taste of the
dish rather than of noodle.

Fifty grams dry is the right amount and it looks like nothing in the packet.
They roughly triple, and 80 g would give you a plate of noodles with some tofu
on it.

Two thirds fried, one third raw. Fried garlic is sweet and nutty and raw garlic
is sharp, and the dish needs both — all fried is bland, all raw is harsh after
eight minutes of steaming. Thirty grams is about ten cloves, and this is a
garlic dish.

Get the fried garlic out of the pan the moment it is gold. Garlic in hot oil
goes from gold to brown to bitter in about ten seconds, and it will keep
browning off the heat if you leave it there.

Cut the noodles dry. This is the single most annoying thing about glass noodles
and it is entirely avoidable.

The scallop version — 蒜蓉粉絲蒸扇貝 — is this exact garlic-and-noodle
arrangement with scallops on the half shell instead of tofu, steamed for 6
minutes. If you ever see cheap scallops, that is what to do with them.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, covered. The noodles keep drinking and by the next day
they are softer and the plate is dry; a tablespoon of water before reheating
fixes it.

Steam for 4 minutes or microwave covered for 90 seconds. Add the raw garlic and
the hot oil fresh if you are reheating a portion — both are gone by then.

It does not freeze; the noodles turn to paste.
