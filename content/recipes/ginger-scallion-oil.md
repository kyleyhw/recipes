## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp ginger, minced
- 6 spring onions, green parts only, finely chopped
- 0.5 tsp salt
- 3 tbsp neutral oil

## Method

1. Put the ginger, spring onion greens and salt in a heatproof bowl — ceramic or metal, not plastic. Push them into a single mound rather than spreading them out.
2. Heat the oil in a small pan over high heat for 2 to 3 minutes, until it shimmers, thins visibly and the first wisp of smoke comes off the surface.
3. Pour it over the mound in one go, from about 10 cm above the bowl. It should hiss hard for 5 seconds and the ginger should turn from pale to translucent. If it only fizzes quietly, the oil was not hot enough — that heat is what cooks the raw edge off the ginger.
4. Stir once and leave for 5 minutes before using.

## Notes

Green parts only. The whites are firmer and stay raw and squeaky at this quantity of oil; keep them for the rice in the ginger and wine chicken.

If the oil catches fire in the pan, put the lid on it and take it off the heat. Do not move the pan and do not use water.

## Storage

Keeps two weeks in the fridge in a sealed jar, and the flavour improves after the first day.

The oil sets solid when cold. Take it out 10 minutes before serving, or spoon it straight onto something hot.

Do not keep it at room temperature. Aromatics under oil are the classic conditions for botulism, and the fridge is the whole precaution.
