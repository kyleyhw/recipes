## Ingredients

- 400 g Chinese cabbage, cut lengthwise into 4 cm pieces, white ribs and green leaves
- 250 g carrots, unpeeled, roughly chopped
- 1 onion, roughly chopped
- 1 apple, peeled and roughly chopped
- 1 kg bone-in chicken thighs
- 100 g ginger, peeled and roughly chopped
- 10 cloves garlic, smashed flat
- 4 bay leaves
- 2 tbsp light soy sauce
- 2 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 1.5 tsp salt
- 1 tsp msg
- 1 tsp white pepper
- 1 tsp granulated sugar

## Method

1. Scrape the skin off the ginger with the edge of a metal teaspoon rather than a peeler — it follows the knobs instead of cutting them off. Chop it into 2 cm chunks.
2. Peel the apple. Chop the apple, carrots and onion into 3 cm pieces. Leave the carrot skins on; they hold their shape through 90 minutes when peeled ones do not.
3. Smash the garlic cloves flat under the side of a knife until they crack. Do not chop them.
4. Whisk the soy sauce, shaoxing wine, salt, msg, white pepper and sugar together in a small bowl for 20 seconds, until the salt and sugar have mostly dissolved. The sugar is there to take the raw edge off the wine, not to sweeten the pot.
5. Put every piece of cabbage in the bottom of a heavy pot with a tight lid, ribs and leaves together. This layer is the water: it sits between the meat and the heat and gives up its liquid slowly.
6. Scatter the carrots, onion and apple evenly over the cabbage.
7. Lay the chicken on top in one layer. Tuck the ginger, garlic and bay leaves into the gaps between the pieces.
8. Pour the seasoning liquid evenly over the chicken.
9. Set the pot over medium-high heat with the lid OFF. When it starts to bubble, boil it uncovered for 5 minutes. This is where the alcohol goes; trap it under a lid now and it stays in the broth for the next hour and a half.
10. Turn the heat to medium-low and put the lid on tightly.
11. Simmer for 60 to 90 minutes without lifting the lid. Every time you look, you lose steam that is not being replaced — there is no water in the pot to spare.
12. Take the pot off the heat. The cabbage and apple should have collapsed into the broth and the chicken should come away from the bone against a spoon. Stir gently once, taste the broth, add salt if it needs it, and serve over rice.

## Notes

The pot must have a lid that actually seals. This whole method is a bet that the cabbage gives up more liquid than the pot loses, and a loose lid loses that bet.

Sixty minutes gives you chicken that holds together; ninety gives you chicken that falls apart. Both are correct — pick one before you start, because you cannot check without opening the lid.

Bone-in only. Boneless thighs go stringy over 90 minutes and give the broth nothing back.

## Storage

Keeps four days in the fridge, and improves on the second day once the broth has set and the fat has risen — lift the cap of fat off before reheating if you want it lighter.

Freezes well for three months. Freeze it in the broth, never drained; the broth is what keeps the meat from drying out.

Reheat gently in a covered pan over low heat for 12 minutes, stirring once. A hard boil shreds the chicken into threads.
