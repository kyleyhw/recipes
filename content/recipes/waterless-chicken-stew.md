## Ingredients

- 400 g Chinese cabbage, roughly chopped
- 250 g carrots, roughly chopped
- 1 onion, roughly chopped
- 1 apple, roughly chopped
- 1 kg bone-in chicken thighs
- 100 g ginger, roughly chopped
- 10 cloves garlic, peeled and smashed flat
- 4 bay leaves
- 1.5 tsp salt
- 1 tsp msg
- 1 tsp white pepper
- 2 tbsp light soy sauce
- 2 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 1 tsp granulated sugar

## Method

1. Chop the cabbage, carrots, onion, apple and ginger into 3 cm pieces. Nothing needs peeling.
2. Smash the garlic cloves flat under the side of a knife until they crack. Do not chop them.
3. Whisk the salt, msg, white pepper, soy sauce, shaoxing wine and sugar together in a small bowl for 20 seconds, until the salt and msg have mostly dissolved. The sugar is there to take the sharp edge off the wine, not to sweeten the pot.
4. Put every piece of cabbage in the bottom of a heavy pot with a tight lid. This layer is the water: it sits between the meat and the heat, keeps everything above it from catching, and gives up the liquid that becomes the broth. Do not put it anywhere else in the pot.
5. Scatter the carrots, onion and apple evenly over the cabbage.
6. Lay the chicken on top in one layer. Tuck the ginger, garlic and bay leaves into the gaps between the pieces.
7. Pour the seasoning liquid evenly over the chicken and put the lid on tightly.
8. Set the pot over medium-low heat and simmer for 60 to 90 minutes without lifting the lid. Every time you look, you lose steam that is not being replaced — there is no water in the pot to spare, and the steam is what melts the cabbage and apple down.
9. Take the pot off the heat. The cabbage and apple should have collapsed into the broth and the chicken should come away from the bone against a spoon. Stir gently once, taste the broth, add salt if it needs it, and serve over rice.

## Notes

The pot must have a lid that actually seals. The whole method is a bet that the cabbage gives up more liquid than the pot loses, and a loose lid loses that bet.

Sixty minutes gives you chicken that holds together; ninety gives you chicken that falls apart. Both are correct — pick one before you start, because you cannot check without opening the lid.

Bone-in only. Boneless thighs go stringy over 90 minutes and give the broth nothing back.

Nothing is boiled off before the lid goes on, so the wine's alcohol stays in the pot and cooks down slowly under it. That is what the sugar is for. If it tastes sharp at the end, it wants another pinch of sugar rather than more salt.

Start at 1.5 tsp of salt. There is no way to taste it before the end, and the broth reduces further on the second day.

## Storage

Keeps four days in the fridge, and improves on the second day once the broth has set and the fat has risen — lift the cap of fat off before reheating if you want it lighter.

Freezes well for three months. Freeze it in the broth, never drained; the broth is what keeps the meat from drying out.

Reheat gently in a covered pan over low heat for 12 minutes, stirring once. A hard boil shreds the chicken into threads.
