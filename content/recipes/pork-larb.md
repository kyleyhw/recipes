## Ingredients

- 500 g pork mince
- 60 ml lime juice
- 5 tbsp fish sauce
- 2 tsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp chilli flakes
- 3 shallots, very thinly sliced
- 20 g mint
- 20 g coriander, leaves and tender stems
- 2 tbsp jasmine rice
- 120 g lettuce, in whole leaves

## Method

1. Put the rice in a dry frying pan over medium heat. Toast for 4 to 5 minutes, shaking the pan every 30 seconds, until the grains are the colour of weak tea and smell of popcorn. Tip onto a plate to cool, then grind to a coarse powder — it should feel like sand, not flour.
2. Whisk the lime juice, fish sauce, sugar and chilli flakes together until no sugar is left on the bottom of the bowl.
3. Put the same pan over medium-high heat with no oil. Add the pork and cook for 7 to 10 minutes, breaking it apart with the edge of a spoon, until the liquid it releases has boiled away and the meat is starting to catch and crisp on the pan.
4. Turn the heat off completely. This matters: the dressing is meant to be absorbed, not boiled, and lime juice cooked hard goes bitter.
5. Pour the dressing over the pork and stir for 20 seconds. Add the shallots, mint, coriander and rice powder, and toss 4 or 5 times — the herbs should be barely wilted and still green.
6. Serve at once with the lettuce leaves and rice.

## Notes

The rice powder is optional in name only. It is what stops the dish being wet mince, and there is no substitute for it.

Five tablespoons of fish sauce reads like an error and is not. It is the salt of the dish; there is none other in it.

Taste before serving and adjust with lime, not salt. It should be sour first, salty second, hot third.

## Storage

Keeps two days in the fridge and is good cold, but the herbs will have gone dark — stir through a fresh handful before serving.

Do not reheat it. Warming it cooks the herbs and turns the dressing bitter; take it out of the fridge 20 minutes ahead instead.
