## Ingredients

- 200 g jellyfish
- 300 g chicken breast
- 20 g ginger, in 5 mm slices
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 200 g cucumber, in 5 cm matchsticks
- 2 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
- 20 g spring onions, finely sliced
- 10 g coriander, roughly chopped

## Method

1. Rinse the jellyfish under the cold tap, then cover it with cold water in a bowl. Leave 10 minutes, drain, and rub the strands between your fingers under running water for 20 seconds. Do that five times in all, which takes about an hour. Then taste a strand: it should taste faintly of the sea and not of salt. If it is still salty, give it two more changes — this is the difference between a dish and a mouthful of brine, and nothing later removes salt.
2. While it soaks, poach the chicken. Put the breast in a 20 cm saucepan with the ginger and the shaoxing wine, cover with cold water by 3 cm, and bring to the very edge of a simmer — the surface trembling, one bubble at a time. Hold it there 12 minutes.
3. Take the pan off the heat, put the lid on, and leave it 10 minutes. The residual heat finishes it, and this is why the meat comes out silky rather than fibrous. A poached breast that has actually boiled is stringy and cannot be rescued.
4. Lift the chicken out, let it cool for 10 minutes, then pull it into strips along the grain with your fingers — 5 cm long and about as thick as a match. Fingers, not a knife: torn edges hold dressing and cut ones shed it.
5. Heat a pan of water to 75 °C. That is steaming hard with small bubbles sitting on the base and none rising — well short of a simmer. If you have no thermometer, boil it and then stand it off the heat for 3 minutes.
6. Drop the drained jellyfish in for 3 seconds and lift it straight out with a sieve. It curls, shrinks by about a third and turns from limp to springy. Three seconds, once. Held longer or in hotter water it seizes into something with the texture of a rubber band, and there is no recovering it.
7. Straight into a bowl of iced water for 5 minutes, then drain it hard and press it between two sheets of kitchen paper. Water left on it dilutes the dressing into a puddle.
8. Whisk the light soy sauce, rice vinegar, sugar and sesame oil until no sugar rasps on the bottom of the bowl.
9. Toss the jellyfish, chicken and cucumber through the dressing with your hands, lifting rather than stirring, until everything is coated. Refrigerate 10 minutes.
10. Serve cold, scattered with the sesame seeds, spring onions and coriander. It should crunch.

## Notes

The whole dish is two temperatures and one clock. The chicken is held below a
simmer and finished off the heat; the jellyfish meets water at 75 °C for three
seconds. Both fail the same way — treated harder they go tough — and both are
unrecoverable, which is why the numbers are numbers rather than adjectives.

The panel on this page says 5,379 mg of sodium a serving and is wrong by about
a factor of ten. It counts 200 g of jellyfish as it comes out of the pack, which
is close to a tenth salt by weight, and the first hour of the recipe is spent
soaking that salt into the sink. What actually reaches the plate is the two
tablespoons of soy sauce — about 450 mg a serving — plus whatever the strands
have kept, which is why step 1 ends by tasting one. This is the same shape of
error as counting frying oil that stays in the pan, and the library cannot know
the difference: it reads a weight and a row, and neither of them knows about the
sink.

Buy the shredded kind (海蜇絲) rather than the sheets (海蜇皮) unless you want to
cut it yourself; the sheets are the better product and want soaking longer and
slicing 5 mm wide. Either comes in a vacuum pack from the chilled cabinet of a
Chinese supermarket, keeps for a year unopened, and costs very little.

Jellyfish tastes of almost nothing, and that is the point rather than a
shortcoming — it is bought for a texture no vegetable can imitate, a crunch that
goes on and on without ever becoming chewing. Everything else in the bowl is
there to give it flavour to carry.

The Shanghai version dresses the same two things with mustard instead: 1 tbsp
of English mustard powder slaked in 1 tbsp of warm water and left 10 minutes to
bloom, whisked into the dressing in place of half the sesame oil. It is
considerably more aggressive and is the version that turns up on a cold platter
at a wedding.

A teaspoon of chilli oil in the dressing is not traditional here and is good.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, covered, and it is honestly better after a few hours
than straight away, because the jellyfish keeps taking on the dressing. What it
loses is the cucumber, which weeps and goes limp overnight — add that fresh on
the second day and the rest is as good as it was.

Drain the bowl before serving it again. Everything in it sheds water in the
fridge and what collects at the bottom is dressing that has been diluted out of
usefulness.

Do not freeze it, and it is worth being plain about why: freezing destroys the
texture of jellyfish completely and permanently, and texture is the only reason
this dish exists.
