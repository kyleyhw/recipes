## Ingredients

- 400 g mixian (2 portions)
- 36 g preserved bean curd (2 cubes and a tablespoon of their brine)
- 12 g garlic, crushed to a paste
- 2 tbsp black vinegar
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 2 tbsp chilli oil
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 200 g cucumber, in fine matchsticks
- 60 g bean sprouts
- 20 g coriander, roughly chopped
- 10 g mint, leaves picked
- 30 g roasted peanuts, crushed
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds

## Method

1. Bring a pan of water to the boil. Drop the bean sprouts in for 20 seconds, lift them straight into cold water, and drain them hard.
2. Put the mixian in a sieve and pour the boiling water over it to loosen and warm it, then run it under cold water until it is cold to the touch. Shake it dry — properly, or the dressing will slide off.
3. Mash the two cubes of fermented bean curd with a tablespoon of their brine and the garlic in a bowl until it is a smooth paste with no lumps. Stir in the black vinegar, soy sauce and sugar until the sugar has gone, then the chilli oil and sesame oil.
4. Put the noodles in a large bowl, pour the dressing over, and toss with your hands until every strand is coated and pale red.
5. Add the cucumber, bean sprouts, coriander and mint and toss once more — briefly, so the herbs stay whole.
6. Divide between two bowls, scatter the peanuts and sesame seeds over, and eat within a few minutes.

## Notes

Fu yu is the dressing. Two cubes of fermented bean curd and a spoon of their
brine give a salty, funky, faintly cheesy depth that no amount of soy sauce
reproduces, and it is what makes this taste Yunnanese rather than like a generic
cold noodle salad. Mash it properly — a lump of it in one mouthful is
overwhelming and the next four taste of nothing.

The 36 g on the ingredient line is the two cubes and the brine together. The
brine is the same product with more water in it, so counting it as more of the
cube is close enough for a panel and simpler than a second line for a
tablespoon of liquid.

Cold noodles, dried hard. Mixian rinsed cold and not shaken out carries a film of
water that dilutes everything; this is the same failure that ruins a cold pasta
salad. Shake the sieve until it stops dripping.

Mint and coriander both, and a lot of them. Yunnan cooking uses herbs by the
handful in a way the rest of China largely does not — this bowl is closer to
northern Thailand or Vietnam than to Sichuan, and the herbs are half the dish
rather than a garnish on it.

No broth at all, which makes this the summer counterpart to Xiaoguo Mixian.
Same noodle, same shop, opposite temperature, and about a fifth of the work.

Shredded poached chicken is the usual addition and turns this into a whole meal
rather than a side; 150 g between two, torn rather than sliced, added at step 5.
It is left out of the list because the dish is complete without it and because a
poached chicken breast is twenty minutes this recipe otherwise does not need.

## Storage

An hour in the fridge, at the outside. The cucumber weeps, the herbs darken and
the noodles go on absorbing the dressing until there is none left loose.

The dressing alone keeps a week in the fridge in a jar and is worth making four
times over — it is good on cold tofu, on blanched greens, and on plain rice.

Nothing here freezes.
