## Ingredients

- 200 g unsalted butter, softened
- 200 g caster sugar
- 2 lemons, zested
- 0.5 tsp salt
- 4 eggs
- 200 g all-purpose flour
- 2 tsp baking powder
- 80 ml lemon juice
- 100 g granulated sugar

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 175 °C. Line a 900 g loaf tin with baking paper standing 2 cm above the rim.
2. Rub the lemon zest into the caster sugar with your fingertips for 30 seconds. The sugar goes damp and pale yellow and the kitchen smells of lemon — the abrasive crystals are tearing the oil out of the zest, and zest stirred in later gives a fraction of this.
3. Beat the butter, lemon sugar and salt with an electric whisk for 4 minutes, until pale and light and holding a soft peak on a lifted beater.
4. Beat in the eggs one at a time, 30 seconds each, adding a tablespoon of the flour with the third and fourth. Without it the batter splits; if it does anyway, another minute of beating brings it back.
5. Sift the remaining flour and the baking powder over the bowl and fold with a spatula until the last dry streak goes, 20 turns. Scrape into the tin and level the top.
6. Bake for 50 minutes. It is done when the crack down the middle is dry to the touch, the cake has come away from the paper at the corners, and a skewer into the centre comes out with crumbs but no wet batter. Start checking at 45 minutes.
7. While it bakes, stir the lemon juice and granulated sugar together in a small bowl. Do not heat it and do not try to dissolve it — the undissolved grains are the crust.
8. Take the cake out and, without moving it from the tin, prick it all over to the base with a skewer, 30 holes. Spoon the lemon mixture slowly over the whole surface, letting each spoonful sink before the next. It looks like far too much liquid and the cake takes all of it.
9. Leave it in the tin for 90 minutes, until completely cold. The syrup travels down as it cools and the sugar left on top dries to a white crust. Lift it out by the paper only when cold; hot, it will break across the middle.

## Notes

Hot cake, cold syrup, and never the other way round. A warm syrup dissolves its
own sugar and soaks in without leaving anything behind, which gives a lemon
cake instead of a lemon drizzle cake — the crust is the entire point and it is
made of grains that never dissolved.

Granulated for the drizzle and caster for the cake. This is the one recipe here
where granulated sugar is chosen for being coarse: caster sugar dissolves in
the juice while you are waiting and gives a glaze rather than a crackle.

Rub the zest into the sugar. It takes thirty seconds and it roughly doubles how
much the cake tastes of lemon, because the flavour is in the oil and the oil
does not come out on its own.

Zest two lemons and you will have juiced them for about 80 ml, which is what
the drizzle takes — so the recipe uses up exactly what it buys. Bottled juice
works and is flatter; if that is what you have, add the zest of a third lemon.

The drizzle needs somewhere to go. Thirty skewer holes right through to the
base is not excessive, and a cake pricked only on top ends up with a wet crown
and a dry bottom half.

## Storage

Five days in a tin at room temperature and better on days two and three than on
day one, as the syrup finishes moving through the crumb. This is the longest
window of any cake in this collection and the sugar is why.

The crust softens once the cake is wrapped, which cannot be avoided and is a
fair trade for the keeping. It never comes back, so if the crust is what you
are there for, eat it the first day.

It freezes for three months, whole or in slices, wrapped tightly. Thaw at room
temperature in the wrapping for two hours; unwrapped it dries out and the top
goes tacky.
