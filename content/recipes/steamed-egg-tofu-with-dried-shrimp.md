## Ingredients

- 15 g dried shrimp
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 15 g garlic, finely chopped
- 10 g ginger, finely chopped
- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 3 tbsp water
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Cover the dried shrimp with warm water and leave them 15 minutes, until they have plumped and gone from hard and brittle to springy. Drain them and throw the water away — it carries a great deal of the salt they were preserved in, and this dish does not need it. Chop them roughly; you want pieces, not a paste.
2. Heat the oil in a small pan over medium heat. Add the chopped shrimp and fry for 2 minutes, stirring, until they have gone a shade darker and the kitchen smells strongly of the sea. This is where all the flavour comes from and it cannot be got by steaming them raw.
3. Add the garlic and ginger and fry 45 seconds more, until the garlic is pale gold.
4. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the soy sauce, sugar, white pepper and water.
5. Slit the tofu tube down its length, slide the tofu out, cut it into rounds 2 cm thick, and lay them overlapping on a heatproof plate with a rim.
6. Spoon the shrimp mixture over, getting some onto every round.
7. Put a rack in a wok or wide pan, add water to just below it, bring to a boil, then drop to a steady simmer. Set the plate on the rack, cover, and steam 8 minutes, until the tofu is hot through and the sauce has run down between the rounds.
8. Scatter the spring onion and serve at once, with rice.

## Notes

Soak them, throw the water away, then fry them. All three matter. Dried shrimp
are preserved in salt — often 2500 mg of sodium per 100 g and sometimes twice
that — and the soaking water is where a good deal of it goes. Frying is what
turns them from something that smells like an aquarium into something that
smells like dinner.

Fifteen grams is about two tablespoons and it is enough to flavour the whole
plate. This is the cheapest big-flavour ingredient in the cupboard and the most
easily overdone: at 30 g the dish tastes of nothing but dried shrimp.

Buy the small pink ones rather than the large pale ones, and keep them in the
fridge rather than the cupboard — see the ingredient note. Grey-brown shrimp
that smell sharp have oxidised and will make the dish taste of ammonia.

**XO sauce** is the shortcut and the upgrade: a tablespoon of it in place of the
shrimp, garlic and ginger entirely, stirred with the soy and water and spooned
over. It is essentially this mixture already made, with dried scallop and chilli
in it, and it costs about fifteen pounds a jar. It is not in the ingredient list
because the published figures for it disagree by a factor of four and this
collection would rather leave a number out than print one it cannot stand
behind — so the panel below would be wrong if you use it, and the sodium in
particular would be much higher.

Do not add salt. Between the shrimp and the soy there is plenty.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, covered, on the plate. Reheat by steaming for 4 minutes
or microwaving covered for 90 seconds; nothing here has a texture that suffers.

The fried shrimp mixture on its own keeps a week in a jar in the fridge under
its oil, and is worth making four times over — it goes on plain rice, into
greens, or over noodles.

It does not freeze once assembled.
