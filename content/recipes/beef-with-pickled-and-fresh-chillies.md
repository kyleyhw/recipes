## Ingredients

- 300 g beef sirloin, sliced 3 mm thick across the grain
- 1 tsp cornflour
- 2 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- 3 tbsp peanut oil
- 20 g ginger, finely chopped
- 6 cloves garlic, finely chopped
- 60 g bird's eye chillies, sliced into rings
- 60 g pickled chillies, roughly chopped
- 60 g coriander, stems and leaves, cut into 4 cm lengths
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp chicken bouillon powder

## Method

1. Put the beef in a bowl with 1/2 tsp of the salt, 1 tbsp of the light soy sauce, the oyster sauce, the cornflour and 1 tbsp of cold water. Stir in one direction for 60 seconds, until the liquid has disappeared into the meat and the slices feel slippery rather than wet.
2. Stir in 1 tbsp of the peanut oil and leave for 10 minutes. The oil is what stops the slices welding together in the wok.
3. Separate the coriander stems from the leaves. Both go in; they go in at different times.
4. Heat 1 tbsp of the peanut oil in a wok over the highest heat until it shimmers and a slice of ginger dropped in sizzles hard, about 90 seconds.
5. Add the beef in one layer. Leave it for 20 seconds without moving it, then stir hard for 40 seconds, breaking up the slices, until no red is left on the outside. Tip it into a bowl. It is not cooked through, and it should not be.
6. Wipe the wok, add the last 1 tbsp of oil, and return it to high heat for 30 seconds.
7. Add the ginger and garlic. Stir for 30 seconds, until they smell sweet and the garlic is pale gold at the edges — no further, or the whole dish turns bitter.
8. Add both chillies and the coriander stems. Stir for 60 seconds, until the fresh chillies have gone from bright to dull red and the pickled ones smell sharp.
9. Pour in the remaining 1 tbsp of light soy sauce down the hot side of the wok, where it will hiss and caramelise for 5 seconds.
10. Return the beef and any juice in the bowl. Add the remaining 1/2 tsp salt and the chicken bouillon powder. Stir for 45 seconds, until every slice is coated and glossy.
11. Throw in the coriander leaves, stir 3 times, and turn it straight out onto a plate. Serve with rice.

## Notes

Take the beef out while the inside is still pink. It finishes in step 10, and beef stir-fried to done in one pass is beef you have to chew twice.

The two chillies do different jobs. Bird's eye brings the heat and nothing else; the pickled ones bring the sour edge that makes it worth eating a second mouthful. Dropping either one makes a flatter dish, not a milder one.

Sixty grams of fresh chillies is not a mistake. Halve it if you must, but halve the pickled ones too, or the balance tips sour.

## Storage

Best within the hour: the coriander wilts and the fresh chilli loses its snap.

Keeps two days in the fridge. Bring it back in a hot dry wok for 60 seconds — long enough to heat through, short enough not to cook the beef a second time. Add a handful of fresh coriander at the end, because the original will have gone grey.
