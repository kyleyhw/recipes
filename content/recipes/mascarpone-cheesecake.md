## Ingredients

- 150 g digestive biscuits (11 biscuits)
- 0.5 tsp ground cinnamon
- 75 g unsalted butter, melted
- 750 g mascarpone (3 tubs), at room temperature
- 175 g caster sugar
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 150 ml sour cream
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 3 eggs, lightly beaten

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 180 °C, or 160 °C fan. Put the biscuits in a freezer bag, press the air out, and crush them with a rolling pin until no piece is bigger than a grain of rice — 2 minutes of steady rolling, not smashing.
2. Stir the crumbs, cinnamon and melted butter together until the mixture holds its shape when squeezed in a fist and looks like wet sand.
3. Tip it into a 23 cm springform tin and press it flat across the base with the bottom of a glass, working from the middle outwards. Press hard: a loose crust falls apart under the knife.
4. Bake for 10 minutes, until it smells of toasted biscuit and the edge has darkened a shade. Set it aside to cool completely — 30 minutes — and turn the oven down to 150 °C, or 130 °C fan.
5. Put the mascarpone in a large bowl and smear it against the side with a spatula for 60 seconds, until it is smooth and has no lumps left in it. Take it no further. Mascarpone splits into grains if it is beaten, and once split it does not come back.
6. Add the sugar and the lemon juice. Stir slowly, folding rather than whipping, for 30 seconds, until no grains rasp against the bowl.
7. Stir in the sour cream and the vanilla until the colour is even, with no paler streaks left.
8. Pour in the beaten eggs and fold them through with a whisk, 15 slow strokes, until no yellow streak remains. Stop there. Every stroke past this point beats air in, and air in a baked cheesecake becomes a crack.
9. Boil a kettle. Put a shallow tray on the bottom shelf of the oven and the cheesecake on the middle shelf, then pour 500 ml of boiling water into the tray and shut the door. That steam does the job of a water bath without a roasting pan, and without the risk of water in the springform.
10. Bake for 55 minutes. It is done when the outer 5 cm is set and the middle 8 cm still wobbles as one piece when you shake the tin — like a set jelly rather than a liquid. It will look underdone. It is not.
11. Turn the oven off and prop the door open with a wooden spoon. Leave the cheesecake there for 1 hour. This is what stops it cracking: the fall from oven heat to room temperature is what tears the surface, and an hour of falling slowly is the whole difference.
12. Take it out, run a thin knife around the inside of the tin to free the edge, and leave it on the counter until it is cold, 2 hours.
13. Refrigerate for at least 8 hours, and better 24, before releasing the tin. Mascarpone sets softer than cream cheese and a cheesecake cut at 4 hours will slump on the plate.

## Notes

Mascarpone rather than cream cheese changes two things and the recipe answers
both. It is richer — 44% fat against about 34% — so there is less sugar here
than a New York cheesecake usually takes. And it is barely sour, where cream
cheese is tangy, which is what the tablespoon of lemon juice is putting back. It
is not there to taste of lemon and you should not be able to find it.

Never beat mascarpone. Smear it, fold it, stir it slowly. It is cream that has
been soured and drained rather than cultured, and it breaks into grains under a
mixer in a way cream cheese does not. That this is mixed by hand is a feature.

The steam tray in place of a roasting pan is the same trick as a water bath and
safer: no pan of hot water to carry, and no water sitting against the seam of a
springform tin, which is where a soggy crust comes from.

The wobble is the only doneness test worth using. A cheesecake baked until the
middle is firm is a cheesecake that will be dry, because it carries on cooking
for twenty minutes after it leaves the oven.

Eight hours is a minimum and not a target. At 24 hours it slices cleanly, the
crust has firmed against the filling, and the flavour has settled — this is a
dessert to make the day before, and it is one of the few things that is
genuinely better for it.

The tin matters. This is written for a 23 cm springform, which the site will
scale with the serving count. Baked in a smaller tin the batter is deeper, the
edges set long before the middle does, and the wobble test stops meaning
anything.

## Storage

Four days in the fridge, covered, in the tin or on a plate under an upturned
bowl. Cling film pressed to the surface marks it, so keep the cover clear of the
top.

It freezes well, which most baked things do not: wrap the whole cake, or single
slices on a tray until firm and then bagged, for 2 months. Defrost overnight in
the fridge and never on the counter, where the outside sweats before the middle
has thawed.

Cut it with a thin knife dipped in hot water and wiped between every slice. A
dry knife drags the filling and gives you twelve ragged edges.
