## Ingredients

- 20 g dried shiitake mushrooms
- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 2 tsp cornflour
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Cover the shiitake with 250 ml of warm water and leave them 20 minutes, until soft all the way through with no hard core. Squeeze them out over the bowl, keeping every drop. Cut the stems off and throw them away, and slice the caps 3 mm thick.
2. Pour the soaking water carefully into a small pan, leaving the last tablespoon behind in the bowl — that is where the grit settles. You want about 200 ml.
3. Slit the tofu tube down its length, slide the tofu out, cut it into rounds 2 cm thick, and lay them overlapping on a heatproof plate with a rim. Scatter the sliced shiitake over.
4. Put a rack in a wok or wide pan, add water to just below it, bring to a boil, then drop to a steady simmer. Set the plate on the rack, cover, and steam 10 minutes, until the tofu is hot through and the mushrooms are glossy.
5. While it steams, add the oyster sauce, soy sauce, sugar and white pepper to the pan of soaking water and bring it to a simmer over medium heat.
6. Stir the cornflour into 2 tablespoons of cold water until smooth, then pour it into the simmering pan in a thin stream, stirring. Cook 30 seconds more, until the gravy coats the back of a spoon and a line drawn through it holds for a second. Stir in the sesame oil.
7. Take the plate out of the steamer and tip off any water that has collected. Pour the gravy over, scatter the spring onion, and serve at once with rice.

## Notes

The soaking water is the stock and throwing it away is the mistake. Twenty grams
of dried shiitake give up more savour into 250 ml of water than any stock cube
will, and this recipe is built around using it rather than around the mushrooms
themselves. Leave the last tablespoon in the bowl: fine grit always settles
there.

Warm water, not boiling. Boiling water softens the outside fast and leaves a
hard core, and it drives off some of what you are trying to extract. Twenty
minutes in warm water gets there properly, and overnight in cold water is better
still if you think of it.

The gravy is made separately and poured over at the end. Steaming it on the
plate would dilute it with everything the tofu sheds, and it would not thicken
evenly on a plate anyway. This way the gravy is the consistency you decided on
and the plate has been drained first.

Dried, not fresh. A fresh shiitake steamed on top of this is watery and mild;
drying concentrates the guanylate that makes them taste of anything, which is
why the dried ones cost more and are worth it here. Fresh shiitake belong in a
stir-fry.

Two teaspoons of cornflour to 200 ml is a light gravy that runs off a spoon.
Take it to three if you want it to sit on the tofu instead.

## Storage

Three days in the fridge, covered. This is the best keeper of the egg tofu
dishes — there is no crust and no raw garnish that matters, and the gravy keeps
the tofu from drying.

Reheat by steaming for 5 minutes or in the microwave covered for 2 minutes. The
gravy thickens as it chills and loosens again with a spoonful of water.

It does not freeze; the tofu comes back spongy and the gravy separates.
