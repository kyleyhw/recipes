## Ingredients

- 120 g fatty pork mince
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 1 tsp cornflour
- 0.5 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 10 g ginger, finely chopped
- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 120 g preserved eggs
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Stir the pork with the soy sauce, shaoxing wine, cornflour, sugar, white pepper and ginger for 45 seconds, until it clings together on the spoon.
2. Peel the two preserved eggs, rinse them, and cut each into 6 wedges. Handle them gently: the yolk at the centre is soft and the wedges fall apart if you saw at them. A knife wiped wet between cuts helps.
3. Slit the tofu tube down its length, slide the tofu out, and cut it into rounds 2 cm thick. Lay them flat on a heatproof plate with a rim, slightly apart.
4. Press a teaspoon of pork onto each round, flattened to about 5 mm, and put the rest in the gaps. Tuck the preserved egg wedges in among them, cut side up, spaced so each portion gets three.
5. Put a rack in a wok or wide pan, add water to just below it, bring it to a boil, then drop to a steady simmer.
6. Set the plate on the rack, cover, and steam 12 minutes. It is done when the pork has turned from pink to pale grey-brown all through and a clear broth has gathered on the plate. The preserved egg will have softened and gone slightly translucent at the edges and darkened towards the centre.
7. Trickle the sesame oil over, scatter the spring onion, and serve on the plate with rice.

## Notes

Cut into wedges, not chopped, and added at the end rather than mixed in. A
preserved egg stirred through mince disappears into it and turns the whole plate
grey-green; left in recognisable pieces it stays what people are eating this
for. This is the same argument as in Preserved Egg and Pork Congee, where half
goes in and half goes on top — here none of it goes in.

Three eggs on one plate and only the tofu contains a fresh one. That is the joke
of the dish and also why it works: the tube is mild and custardy, the preserved
egg is dark and sulphurous, and the pork sits between them. Take any one away
and it is an ordinary plate.

Steamed rather than raw is worth knowing about if preserved egg is not yet your
thing. Twelve minutes takes a good deal of the ammonia edge off and softens the
yolk into something closer to a rich custard — this is a gentler introduction
than the cold sliced version with pickled ginger.

Less pork than the plain minced version, deliberately: 120 g rather than 150,
because the preserved egg is already doing half the work and a full load of pork
crowds it out.

A steady simmer, as always with this tube. Hard steam pits the tofu.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, covered, on the plate. The preserved egg firms up cold
and softens again on reheating.

Steam for 5 minutes or microwave covered for 2. Nothing here loses anything —
this is one of the more forgiving dishes on the site, having been steamed to
begin with.

It does not freeze. Egg tofu thaws spongy and preserved egg goes rubbery.
