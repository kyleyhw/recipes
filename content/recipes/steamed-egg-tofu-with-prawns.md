## Ingredients

- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 150 g prawns, peeled and deveined
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 10 g ginger, in fine slivers
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 2 tbsp water
- 1 tsp sesame oil

## Method

1. Snip one end off the tube, slit it down its length, and let the tofu slide out in one piece. Cut it into rounds 2 cm thick and lay them along a heatproof plate with a rim, overlapping like fallen dominoes. There is no pressing to do here — that is only for frying, and this dish wants the water.
2. Butterfly the prawns: cut down the back most of the way through so each opens flat. Toss them with the shaoxing wine and leave them while the steamer heats.
3. Put a rack in a wok or wide pan, add water to just below it, and bring to a boil. Turn it down to a steady simmer rather than a rolling one.
4. Tuck the prawns between and over the tofu rounds, cut side up. Set the plate on the rack, cover, and steam for 8 minutes. It is done when the prawns have turned opaque white all through and curled tight, and the tofu is hot to a finger laid on it.
5. Take the plate out and tip off the liquid that has collected — hold the tofu back with a spoon. It is mostly water out of the tofu and the prawns, and left there it turns the dressing grey and thin.
6. Whisk the soy sauce, sugar, water and sesame oil in a cup until the sugar has gone, and spoon it over the plate.
7. Pile the ginger and spring onion on top. Heat the neutral oil in a small pan over high heat for about 90 seconds, until it shimmers hard and the first wisp of smoke lifts off it, then pour it straight over the ginger and spring onion. It should hiss and the smell should change at once. Serve immediately, with rice.

## Notes

A steady simmer, not a rolling boil. Egg tofu is already a set custard, and hard
steam makes it weep and pit — the rounds go from smooth to spongy and a puddle
gathers. Gentle heat for eight minutes cooks the prawns and leaves the tofu
alone, which is the whole balance of the dish.

Pour the water off. Step 5 looks fussy and is the difference between a dressing
that clings and one that sits in a grey pool. It is worth doing over the sink
with the plate tilted.

The oil has to be properly smoking. This is the same finish as a Cantonese
steamed fish, and it exists to cook the raw ginger and spring onion in the two
seconds it takes to hit them — oil that is merely hot leaves them raw and tastes
greasy. Ninety seconds over high heat in a small pan is enough; stand back when
you pour.

Butterfly the prawns rather than leaving them whole. Split, they cook in the
same eight minutes the tofu needs and they curl into a shape that holds the
dressing. Whole ones need longer and by then the tofu has suffered.

The panel reads high in sodium and most of it is not the soy sauce — see the
prawns row, which explains why almost all peeled prawns carry four times the
sodium of an untreated one. If you can buy shell-on and peel them yourself, this
dish drops by several hundred milligrams a serving and tastes better.

Dried scallops, soaked and shredded, or a spoonful of XO sauce added at step 6
are the two upgrades. Steamed Egg Custard is the same idea with the tofu made
from scratch in the dish.

## Storage

Eat it straight away. The oil finish is at its best in the first minute and the
tofu keeps weeping as it stands.

A day in the fridge, covered, and it is fine cold — better cold, in fact, than
reheated, since a second steaming finishes off the prawns. If you must warm it,
2 minutes covered in the microwave and accept the prawns will be firm.

It does not freeze.
