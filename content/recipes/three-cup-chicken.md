## Ingredients

- 800 g chicken thighs, boneless and cut into 3 cm chunks
- 2 tbsp sesame oil
- 40 g ginger (a thumb), sliced thinly
- 6 cloves garlic, peeled and left whole
- 3 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 3 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp granulated sugar
- 30 g thai basil (a large handful)

## Method

1. Heat the sesame oil in a wide pan over medium heat for 60 seconds, until it smells nutty.
2. Add the ginger and the whole garlic cloves. Fry for 4 minutes, turning them, until both are golden and the garlic has softened enough to dent under a spoon. Toasted sesame oil burns easily — if it starts to smoke, take the pan off the heat for 20 seconds.
3. Turn the heat to high and add the chicken in one layer. Fry for 4 minutes, turning once, until no pink shows on the outside.
4. Pour in the wine, soy sauce and sugar. Bring to a boil, then turn the heat to low, cover, and simmer for 10 minutes.
5. Take the lid off and turn the heat back to high. Boil hard for 5 to 7 minutes, moving the chicken through the sauce, until the liquid has gone from a pool to a dark syrup that coats the back of a spoon and leaves a clear trail when you drag one across the pan.
6. Turn off the heat. Throw in the basil, stir twice — it should wilt in the residual heat and stay green — and serve at once with rice.

## Notes

The garlic goes in whole and stays whole. Chopped, it disappears into the sauce; whole, it turns sweet and is the best thing on the plate.

Take the reduction further than feels comfortable. The dish is named for its three liquids, and if any of them is still liquid at the end it is not finished.

Thai basil, not sweet basil. Sweet basil goes grey and tastes of aniseed against the soy.

## Storage

Keeps three days in the fridge and reheats better than most stir-fries, because the sauce is already a syrup.

Reheat in a pan over medium heat for 4 minutes with 1 tbsp of water to loosen it. Add fresh basil at the end; the original will have blackened.

Freezes for two months without the basil.
