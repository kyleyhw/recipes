## Ingredients

- 400 g firm tofu (1 block)
- 200 ml dashi
- 3 tbsp light soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 3 tbsp cornflour
- 10 g ginger, finely grated
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced
- 3 g katsuobushi

## Method

1. Wrap the tofu block in a folded tea towel, sit it on a plate, and put a second plate and a tin on top. Leave 30 minutes. It will give up two or three tablespoons of water and the towel will be soaked.
2. Warm the dashi, soy sauce and mirin in a small pan until it steams, then keep it warm off the heat. Do not boil it.
3. Cut the pressed tofu into 6 rectangles. Pat every face dry with kitchen paper — properly, until the paper comes away barely marked. Wet tofu spits in the oil and the coating slides off it.
4. Heat 400 ml of neutral oil in a small deep pan to 180°C. A cube of bread should brown in about 40 seconds. The oil is not in the ingredient list above and not in the panel — see the notes.
5. Put the cornflour on a plate and turn each piece in it, pressing lightly so it takes an even coat, then tap off everything loose. A thick powdery layer goes gummy in the dashi.
6. Fry three pieces at a time for 4 minutes, turning once, until the coating is pale gold and firm and rattles faintly against the spoon. It does not brown much and it should not — this is not tempura.
7. Lift onto a rack, not paper. Fry the second batch.
8. Pour the warm dashi into two shallow bowls, sit three pieces in each so the liquid comes a third of the way up, and pile the ginger, spring onion and katsuobushi on top. Take it to the table immediately.

## Notes

This dish is a countdown. The crust is crisp for about ninety seconds after it
meets the liquid and then it is not, and that is the design rather than a fault
— agedashi tofu is meant to be eaten in the state where the top is still crisp
and the bottom has gone soft and soaked. Assemble it at the table if you can,
and never in advance.

Which is why the dashi goes in the bowl and not over the tofu. Poured over, it
wets the top as well and the whole piece is soft before it arrives. A third of
the way up is the right depth.

Press it, and dry it again after cutting. Firm tofu holds a surprising amount of
water, and every bit still in it turns to steam behind the coating and pushes it
off. The thirty minutes under a plate is the difference between a crust and a
loose jacket.

The tsuyu is about four parts dashi to one of soy and one of mirin, which is
stronger than the steeping liquid in Ohitashi and weaker than a noodle dipping
sauce. Made in that ratio it is also what you would pour over cold tofu, or
grilled aubergine, or blanched greens.

Cornflour rather than potato starch. Katakuriko is what a Japanese kitchen uses
and gives a slightly lighter, more brittle shell; cornflour is in every cupboard
and the difference is small enough that nobody would name it blind.

Grated daikon, mounded on top with the ginger, is the classic finish and is
worth it if you have one. It is not in the list because a whole daikon for two
tablespoons is a lot of daikon.

The frying oil is not in the ingredient list and not in the panel, which is the
same decision Mapo Tofu makes about the salt in its blanching water: four
hundred millilitres of oil counted in full would report two thousand calories a
serving for a dish of tofu, and almost all of it goes back in the bottle. What
the panel therefore understates is the oil the coating actually absorbs, which
for fried tofu is roughly 5 to 8% of its weight — call it 25 g between two, or
about 110 kcal a serving that is real and is not shown. Both numbers are wrong
and this is the less wrong one.

## Storage

None, honestly. This is the least keepable thing in the collection: the crust is
finished within two minutes and reheating fried tofu that has sat in liquid
gives something with the texture of a wet sponge.

The tsuyu keeps three days in the fridge and is worth making a double batch of
while the pan is out — it is the same liquid Ohitashi and a bowl of cold tofu
both want.

Pressed, uncooked tofu keeps two days submerged in fresh water in the fridge, so
step 1 can be done the day before.
