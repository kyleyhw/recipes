## Ingredients

- 150 g salted butter
- 100 g brown sugar
- 100 g golden syrup
- 0.25 tsp salt
- 250 g rolled oats

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 170 °C. Line a 20 cm square tin with baking paper, letting it stand above two opposite sides so you can lift the slab out later.
2. Melt the butter, sugar, golden syrup and salt together in a 20 cm saucepan over low heat, stirring, for 4 minutes. It is ready when the sugar has gone and the mixture is glossy and runs off the spoon in a single sheet. Do not let it boil: boiled syrup sets hard and gives a flapjack you cannot bite.
3. Take it off the heat and stir in the oats for 30 seconds, until every flake is coated and there is no dry oat left at the bottom.
4. Tip it into the tin and press it flat and even with the back of a spoon. Press firmly at the corners, which is where it will otherwise be loose and crumble.
5. Bake for 25 minutes. It is done when the outer 2 cm has gone a deep bubbling brown and the middle is still pale gold and looks soft. It firms as it cools and taking it further gives a hard slab rather than a chewy one.
6. Score it into 12 squares as soon as it comes out, cutting most of the way through with a sharp knife.
7. Leave it in the tin for 90 minutes, until completely cold, then lift the slab out by the paper and cut through the scores. Warm flapjack falls apart; there is nothing holding it together but set syrup, and the syrup has to set.

## Notes

Golden syrup, not honey and not maple. It is inverted sugar, which means it
does not crystallise, and that is why a flapjack stays chewy for a week instead
of going grainy after two days. Honey behaves differently and burns at a lower
temperature.

Do not let the butter and sugar boil. Every ruined flapjack is a boiled one:
the syrup goes past the soft-ball stage, and what should be chewy sets to
toffee with oats in it. Low heat, four minutes, stirring.

Rolled oats — porridge oats — and preferably the jumbo ones. Instant oats are
milled thin and dissolve into a paste, which makes something closer to a
biscuit. Steel-cut oats stay raw and will break a tooth.

Underbake it. The middle should look wet coming out of the oven. This is the
same instruction as the one in the Chocolate Chip Cookies and for the same
reason: sugar continues to set for a long time after the heat goes off, and the
only thing you control is how far it has gone before it starts.

Additions that work at 75 g, stirred in with the oats: sultanas, chopped dried
apricots, or desiccated coconut. Chocolate belongs on top rather than in — melt
100 g and spread it over the cooled slab before cutting.

Salted butter here rather than unsalted, which is the opposite of everything
else baked in this collection. There is no structure to protect and a lot of
sugar to cut through, and a quarter-teaspoon of extra salt on top of it is
still not too much.

## Storage

A week in an airtight tin at room temperature, and genuinely better on day two
than on day one — the syrup finishes setting and the oats soften into it.

Do not refrigerate. Cold flapjack is hard and waxy and the texture does not
recover fully at room temperature.

It freezes for three months, cut, with paper between the squares, and thaws in
30 minutes on the counter. Frozen squares are also the easiest thing in this
collection to put in a bag on the way out of the door; they are edible before
you get anywhere.
