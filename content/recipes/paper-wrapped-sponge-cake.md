## Ingredients

- 4 egg yolks
- 50 ml neutral oil
- 50 ml milk
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 90 g all-purpose flour
- 0.25 tsp salt
- 4 egg whites
- 0.5 tsp lemon juice
- 90 g caster sugar

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 170 °C. Stand 8 paper cases 7 cm across and 5 cm deep in a muffin tin, or make your own by wrapping 20 cm squares of baking paper around a tin can and folding the base flat.
2. Whisk the egg yolks, oil, milk and vanilla in a large bowl for 30 seconds, until the oil has stopped sitting in beads on the surface and the mixture is a uniform pale yellow.
3. Sift the flour and salt over it and whisk for 20 seconds, until just smooth. Stop as soon as the lumps go — this is the only gluten in the recipe and there is no butter to shorten it.
4. In a spotlessly clean bowl, whisk the egg whites with the lemon juice on medium until they are opaque and foaming, about 1 minute. Add the caster sugar in three lots, 20 seconds of whisking between each, then whisk on high for 2 minutes more.
5. Stop at soft-to-medium peaks: a lifted whisk carries a peak that stands up and then curls over at the tip. Stiff, dry peaks are the classic failure here — they will not fold in without breaking, and the cake bakes with tunnels through it.
6. Fold a third of the whites into the yolk batter with a spatula to loosen it; be rough with this third. Fold in the rest in two lots, cutting down through the middle and lifting up the side, 15 turns each. Stop while the batter is streak-free and still holds a mounded ribbon.
7. Divide between the cases, filling each to 2 cm below the rim — a level 60 g each. Drop the tin onto the worktop twice from a height of 5 cm to knock out the large bubbles.
8. Bake for 20 minutes. They rise 3 cm above the paper and split open across the top, which is what they are supposed to do. They are done when the split has gone dry and pale gold and a skewer into the centre through the split comes out clean.
9. Take them out and drop the tin onto the worktop once, hard, from 20 cm. This shocks the structure and stops the crumb collapsing as it cools.
10. Cool in the tin for 5 minutes, then on a rack for 40 minutes. They shrink back to the height of the paper as they go, which is normal. Do not peel the paper off; it is how they are sold and how they keep.

## Notes

Chiffon, not sponge, despite the name — the oil is what separates the two.
Butter is solid at room temperature and a butter cake sets firm as it cools; oil
never sets, so the crumb stays damp and soft on the third day. That is the
whole reason this cake exists in a climate like Hong Kong's.

The paper is not packaging. It is the tin: the batter has to grip something to
climb, and a chiffon poured into a greased tin cannot get up the sides. Never
grease the cases, and never use a foil case, which is too slippery.

Soft peaks, and this is the step to get right. The instruction everywhere is
"stiff peaks", and it is wrong for a batter this loose — over-whipped whites
are dry and break into curds when folded, and the cake bakes with tunnels and a
dense seam at the bottom. A peak that flops at the tip folds in cleanly.

Lemon juice is doing what cream of tartar does: acid stabilises the foam so it
survives the folding. Half a teaspoon is enough and any more can be tasted.

The split down the top is a feature. It is where the crust set before the cake
had finished rising, and a bakery version with a smooth dome has usually been
baked cooler and longer and is drier for it.

This is what the steamed Ma Lai Gao is to a steamer: the plain, everyday cake
of the same shop window. They are worth reading together, since one is leavened
by whipped egg and the other by yeast and time.

## Storage

Three days at room temperature in a sealed box, still in their papers, and they
are as good on day two as on day one — the oil sees to that. This is the
longest-keeping cake in the collection and it needs nothing done to it.

Do not refrigerate them. A chiffon crumb goes firm and dry in a fridge within
hours, and it does not come back at room temperature.

They freeze for a month, wrapped individually, and thaw in an hour on the
counter. Ten seconds in a microwave is the one legitimate use of a microwave in
this collection: it puts the softness straight back into a defrosted one.
