## Ingredients

- 1200 ml water
- 40 g ginger, sliced 2 mm thick
- 600 g sweet potato (4 medium), in 3 cm chunks
- 90 g brown sugar

## Method

1. Put the water in a pot that holds at least 2 litres. Slice the ginger 2 mm thick without peeling it, and smack each slice once with the flat of a knife to split the fibres. Add it to the water.
2. Bring to a boil, cover, and simmer for 10 minutes. The water should smell strongly of ginger and taste hot at the back of the throat. This step is the whole flavour of the dish and it happens before anything else goes in.
3. Meanwhile peel the sweet potatoes and cut them into 3 cm chunks. Rinse the chunks in a colander under cold water until the water runs clear, which takes about 20 seconds and keeps the syrup from going cloudy.
4. Add the sweet potato. Simmer uncovered for 20 minutes, without stirring more than twice — stirring breaks the chunks up. It is ready when a skewer goes into a chunk with no resistance at all but the chunk still holds its shape.
5. Now add the sugar, and not before. Simmer for 5 minutes more, until it has dissolved and the syrup has darkened a shade and thinned slightly on the spoon.
6. Serve hot in bowls, three or four chunks and plenty of syrup each, with a slice of the ginger if you like it.

## Notes

The sugar goes in last and this is the one rule. Sugar in the water early draws
water out of the sweet potato and firms its pectin, and the chunks then stay
faintly hard in the middle no matter how long you boil them — an hour will not
fix it. Cook them soft in plain ginger water first, sweeten afterwards.

Forty grams of ginger for four bowls is about a thumb and a half, and it should
be assertive enough to catch at the back of your throat. This is a dish eaten in
winter and after being caught in the rain, and a timid one is pointless. Old
ginger with dry papery skin is stronger than young stem ginger; leave the skin
on, since most of the heat is just under it.

Chinese slab sugar is what this traditionally uses — dark amber blocks of
unrefined cane sugar sold in Asian grocers. Ninety grams of it is two small
slabs, and it brings a faint caramel and molasses note. Brown sugar is the
supermarket answer and gets most of the way there; white sugar gives a cleaner,
flatter, less interesting bowl.

Orange-fleshed sweet potato is sweeter and collapses sooner; the white or purple
kind holds its shape better and tastes starchier. Both are correct and the
orange one is what a supermarket sells. Cut the chunks big — 3 cm, not 2 — since
they soften more than you expect.

Things that go in with the ginger, one at a time: a knotted pandan leaf, a
handful of dried longan, or a few slices of taro alongside the sweet potato,
which is the other classic version.

Half a bowl of this is a good breakfast on a cold morning, which is a use for it
nobody mentions.

## Storage

Four days in the fridge in its syrup, covered, and it is better on the second
day — the ginger keeps going into the sweet potato and the syrup thickens
slightly.

Reheat in a pan over medium heat for 5 minutes, or in the microwave for 2. It
does not suffer at all, being boiled fruit and sugar water to begin with.

Freezes for two months. The chunks come back a little softer at the edges, which
in this dish does not matter.
