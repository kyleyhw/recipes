## Ingredients

- 400 g cucumber (2 short ones)
- 1 tbsp gochugaru
- 9 g garlic, crushed to a paste
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced
- 2 tsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 2 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.25 tsp salt
- 2 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds

## Method

1. Slice the cucumbers into rounds about 3 mm thick. Thin enough to bend, thick enough to snap — a mandoline set fine makes them too thin, and they go limp in the bowl before they reach the table.
2. Put every other ingredient except the sesame seeds into a mixing bowl and stir until the sugar has dissolved and the gochugaru has bloomed from dry flakes into a wet red paste. This takes about 30 seconds of stirring, and the colour change is obvious.
3. Add the cucumber and toss with your hands until each round is red. Twenty seconds is enough.
4. Tip into a serving bowl, scatter the sesame seeds over, and take it straight to the table. It is at its best now.

## Notes

This is the deliberate opposite of [smashed cucumber salad](/recipes/smashed-cucumber-salad), and worth having both. There the cucumber is salted and drained for fifteen minutes so the dressing stays thick; here it is sliced thin, not drained, and dressed at the last second so it stays crunchy and the weeping is part of the sauce. By the time you are halfway down the bowl there is a pink, sharp, slightly watery liquid at the bottom, and that liquid is correct. Spoon it over rice.

The trade is time. This has no fifteen-minute wait, which is why it is the one to make when the rest of dinner is already on the table — but it also has no tolerance for standing, and a bowl left dressed for twenty minutes is a bowl of pink cucumber soup.

Gochugaru is not chilli powder. It is coarse, bright red, sweet and only mildly hot, and this salad uses a whole tablespoon of it — which would be violent with the fine Sichuan chilli powder elsewhere in this collection, and would also be brown. If gochugaru is what you cannot find, use half the amount of ordinary chilli flakes and know the colour will not be right.

Korean recipes often use fish sauce or soup soy here instead of the light soy sauce. Fish sauce, at the same quantity, makes it deeper and slightly funky and is the more traditional answer; both are good and the choice is a mood.

## Storage

Eat it now. This is the salad whose entire design assumes it will be eaten within
a few minutes of dressing, and there is no version of it that keeps.

Leftovers within the same meal are fine. Leftovers overnight are a quick pickle —
soft, sour, red, perfectly edible with rice or in a fried rice, and no longer
this dish.

It does not freeze.
