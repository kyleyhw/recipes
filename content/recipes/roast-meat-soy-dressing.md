## Ingredients

- 60 ml light soy sauce
- 60 ml water
- 2 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.5 tsp dark soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil

## Method

1. Put the light soy, water, sugar and dark soy in the smallest pan you own. Stir once.
2. Bring to a bare simmer over medium heat and hold it there for 60 seconds, until the sugar has gone and the smell has turned from sharp and raw to round and savoury. Do not let it boil hard — reducing it makes it salty rather than better.
3. Take it off the heat and stir in the sesame oil. It should be the colour of strong tea and run off a spoon like water, not cling to it.
4. Spoon it over rice while it is warm, about a tablespoon a plate.

## Notes

This is what a Cantonese roast meat shop puts over a rice plate — over char siu,
over roast pork, over a steamed pork patty. The shop needs it because a patty
steamed in bulk and boxed loses the broth it made; at home you still have that
broth, so the best version of this sauce is made with it.

Use the broth from the dish in place of the water. Tip it out of the steaming
dish into the pan, make up the difference with water if there is not enough, and
carry on from step 1. It is the same sauce with the pork already in it.

The dark soy is for colour and nothing else. Half a teaspoon takes it from thin
brown to the near-black of a shop's squeeze bottle, and more starts to taste of
molasses.

Shops often slacken it with cornflour so that it clings to the rice instead of
running to the bottom of the box. Half a teaspoon mixed with a tablespoon of
cold water, stirred in at the end of step 2, does it. Worth doing for a takeaway
box and not for a plate.

A slice of ginger and the white of a spring onion, simmered in step 2 and fished
out, make a rounder sauce and are how most shops do it. They also make it a
thing you cook rather than a thing you stir, which is the whole reason this
recipe is two minutes long.

Lee Kum Kee's seasoned soy sauce for seafood is the nearest bottle, and it is a
perfectly good shortcut. It is formulated for steamed fish, so it is thinner and
less sweet than this and has no dark soy in it — over rice it reads as soy
rather than as dressing.

## Storage

Two weeks in the fridge in a sealed jar. It is mostly soy sauce, so what fades
first is the sesame oil rather than anything spoiling.

Serve it warm. It thickens very slightly in the cold, and 20 seconds in a pan or
10 in a microwave brings it back.
