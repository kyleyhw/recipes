## Ingredients

- 400 g firm tofu
- 75 ml light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
- 75 ml water
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1.5 tbsp granulated sugar
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 6 cloves garlic, minced
- 80 g onion (half a small one), finely diced
- 4 spring onions, thinly sliced
- 3 bird's eye chillies, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds

## Method

1. Bring 1 litre of water to a boil in a 20 cm saucepan with 1 tsp of salt. Slide the block of tofu in whole and simmer for 5 minutes. The surface firms and the raw, beany smell it has out of the packet goes.
2. Lift the block out with a slotted spoon onto a plate lined with kitchen paper. Leave it 10 minutes, until it has stopped steaming and the paper beneath it is wet.
3. Cut it into 2.5 cm cubes — 24 of them from a 400 g block.
4. Whisk the light and dark soy sauces, the water, the rice vinegar and the sugar in a bowl for 30 seconds, until no grains rasp on the bottom of the bowl when the whisk passes over it.
5. Stir in the sesame oil, garlic, onion, spring onions, chillies and sesame seeds. The marinade should smell sharply of raw garlic — that softens overnight into the background of the dish.
6. Pack the tofu into a container it only just fits, pour the marinade over, and press a sheet of cling film flat onto the surface so that every cube sits under liquid.
7. Refrigerate for 12 hours, turning the container over after the first hour. It is edible at 2 hours, when the outside of each cube has taken colour and the middle is still white; it is right at 12, when the colour has gone the whole way through.
8. Serve cold or at room temperature over hot rice, with a spoonful of the marinade and its aromatics over each portion.

## Notes

Six cloves is not a typo. The garlic goes in raw and spends the night losing its edge; at three you taste the soy and nothing else the next day.

Three bird's eye chillies give a heat you notice on the second cube rather than the first. Take it to five if you want it forward.

The panel says 1400 mg of sodium a serving, and that assumes you eat every drop of the marinade. Most of it stays in the container: lifting the cubes out and spooning over one tablespoon of what they sat in is nearer 900 mg, and is how this is actually eaten. The salt in the blanching water is not counted at all, because it goes down the drain with the water.

The crispy version — cubes fried golden and then dressed, rather than steeped — is a different dish and a much worse keeper: excellent for twenty minutes, soft by the next day. This one is written to be made in advance.

The marinade will do a second job. Six halved soft-boiled eggs put into what is left over, refrigerated for a day, are mayak eggs, which is where the dish comes from.

## Storage

Four days in the fridge, in its marinade, in a sealed container. It is at its best between 12 and 48 hours; after that the spring onions dull and the garlic gets louder rather than softer.

It is eaten cold, so there is nothing to reheat. Do not freeze it: tofu comes out of a freezer spongy and riddled with holes, and it does not come back.
