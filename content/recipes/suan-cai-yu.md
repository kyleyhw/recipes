## Ingredients

- 600 g white fish fillets
- 1 egg white
- 1 tbsp cornflour
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 300 g suan cai yu kit (1 packet)
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 15 g garlic, finely chopped
- 15 g ginger, in fine slivers
- 1200 ml water
- 10 g bird's eye chillies, sliced into rings
- 20 g spring onions, finely sliced
- 10 g coriander, roughly chopped
- 1 tbsp sichuan peppercorn oil

## Method

1. Open the packet and lay the sachets out. There will be three: a bag of pickled mustard greens, a seasoning paste or oil, and a small marinade powder. Brands vary in what they call them; the greens are obvious, the marinade is the smallest.
2. Slice the fish on a steep diagonal into pieces 5 mm thick and about 5 cm across, holding the knife almost flat to the board. Press the slices between kitchen paper — a wet fillet will not hold a coating.
3. Put the fish in a bowl with the marinade sachet, the egg white, cornflour, shaoxing wine and white pepper. Turn it over gently with your hands until every slice is slippery and coated. Leave it while you make the broth. Do not stir it hard; the slices break.
4. Drain the pickled greens and squeeze them out. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium-high heat, add the garlic and ginger and fry 45 seconds, then the greens and the seasoning paste, and fry 3 minutes until the smell turns from sharply sour to deep and savoury and the oil has coloured.
5. Pour in the water, bring to a boil, and simmer 8 minutes. Taste it. It should be aggressively sour and salty on its own, because 600 g of unseasoned fish is about to go in.
6. Turn the heat down until the surface is barely moving. Slide the fish in a slice at a time, spreading them out. Do not stir. Leave them 90 seconds, then push them apart gently with the back of a ladle and cook 90 seconds more.
7. It is done when every slice has turned from translucent to opaque white and a piece breaks along its flakes under a spoon. Three minutes total, and overcooked fish here is the only way to ruin the dish.
8. Slide the whole thing into a deep serving bowl. Scatter the chilli, spring onion and coriander over the top. Heat the sichuan peppercorn oil in a small pan over high heat for 45 seconds, until it shimmers hard, and pour it over the garnish so it hisses. Take it to the table at once, with rice.

## Notes

The packet is the recipe and there is no apology to make for it. Suan cai yu
depends on properly fermented Sichuan pickled mustard greens and on a chilli and
peppercorn seasoning that would take a dozen ingredients to build, and the
packet — Baijia's 300 g one is the easiest to find in Britain, at a little over
two pounds, and Haorenjia, Qiaotou and Tianwei make the same thing — is what
households in Sichuan use. Buying the components separately costs more,
takes an hour, and is not obviously better.

Three minutes for the fish, and this is the only difficult thing here. It goes
into liquid that is barely moving, not a simmer, and it is done the moment it
turns opaque. Boiling it, or stirring it, breaks 5 mm slices into shreds and
turns them woolly.

Egg white and cornflour, and gently. That coating is what keeps the slices
whole and silky in a boiling-hot sour broth — it is the same velveting as the
beef in Beef with Pickled and Fresh Chillies. Mix it with your hands and stop
as soon as everything is coated.

Basa or pangasius is what this is for and what a Chinese supermarket freezer
sells. It is cheap, firm and mild, and it slices well half-frozen. Tilapia is
the same job. Sea bass is better fish and a waste here, since the broth is doing
all the talking. The traditional grass carp is not sold in Britain.

Fry the greens before the water goes in. Three minutes in hot oil is what turns
the packet from something that smells like a jar into something that smells like
a dish, and skipping it leaves the broth thin and one-dimensionally sour.

Taste the broth before the fish goes in and expect it to be too strong. It is
about to be diluted by 600 g of fish and served with rice, and a broth balanced
at step 5 is a bland bowl at step 8.

The panel is the least certain in this collection, and the ingredient row says
why: no manufacturer publishes a verifiable nutrition panel for these packets,
so its figures are constructed from what is in one. The sodium is unambiguously
high whichever way it is measured — a packet is mostly salt and pickle — and
most of the broth is left in the bowl, which the panel cannot know.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, and the fish suffers more than the broth: it firms up
and goes slightly cottony. Eat the fish on the day and keep the broth.

The broth alone keeps four days and freezes for two months, and it is worth
keeping — it will poach another 600 g of fish, or a block of silken tofu, with
nothing added.

Reheat gently to a bare simmer. Boiling leftover suan cai yu destroys what is
left of the fish.
