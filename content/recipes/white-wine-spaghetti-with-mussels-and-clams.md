## Ingredients

- 400 g spaghetti
- 700 g clams in the shell, scrubbed
- 450 g mussels in the shell, scrubbed
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter
- 2 shallots, thinly sliced
- 8 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 1 tsp chilli flakes
- 180 ml dry white wine
- 1 tsp salt
- 4 tbsp flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- 2 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Method

1. Tip the clams and mussels into a bowl of cold water. Throw out any with a cracked shell, and any open one that does not close within 30 seconds of a sharp tap on the counter. Pull the beard from each mussel by gripping it and yanking towards the hinge. Drain.
2. Bring 4 litres of water to a rolling boil with the salt. Use no more salt than that: the shellfish release their own liquor into the sauce, and it is seawater.
3. Heat the olive oil and half the butter in a 28 cm pan with a lid, over medium heat, for 2 minutes, until the butter foams and the foam falls back.
4. Add the shallots. Cook for 3 minutes, stirring, until they are translucent at the edges and still pale.
5. Add the garlic and chilli flakes. Cook for 60 seconds, until the garlic smells sweet and the slices are gold at the rim only. Take it no further — browned garlic turns the whole sauce bitter, and there is no fixing it.
6. Pour in the wine and raise the heat to high. Boil hard for 90 seconds, until the sharp smell of raw alcohol has gone and only the wine's fruit is left.
7. Add the clams, cover, and cook for 3 minutes. Add the mussels, cover again, and cook for 3 minutes more, shaking the pan twice. Every shell should be open. Throw out any that is still shut after 6 minutes in total.
8. While the shellfish cook, drop the spaghetti into the boiling water and cook it for 2 minutes less than the packet says. It finishes in the pan.
9. Lift the shellfish out with a slotted spoon into a bowl, leaving every drop of liquid in the pan. Cover the bowl.
10. Reserve 250 ml of the pasta water, then drain the spaghetti and tip it into the pan with 120 ml of that water. Cook over high heat for 2 minutes, tossing constantly, until the liquid goes from thin and cloudy to glossy and clinging to the strands. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if it tightens before the 2 minutes are up.
11. Take the pan off the heat. Add the remaining butter and toss for 30 seconds, until it has melted into the sauce and the strands shine.
12. Add the lemon juice and parsley, then return the shellfish and every drop of liquid from their bowl. Toss twice and serve at once, with the lemon wedges.

## Notes

Use a wine you would drink. It is a third of the sauce, and a thin, sweet cooking wine makes a thin, sweet sauce.

A teaspoon of chilli flakes is enough to feel and not enough to taste as heat. Take it to two if you want it to bite.

The 2 minutes of tossing in step 10 is the dish. Starch from the pasta water and butter emulsify into the shellfish liquor there; skip it and you have spaghetti sitting in a puddle.

## Storage

Eat it immediately. Shellfish reheated is shellfish made rubbery, and the sauce breaks on standing.

If there is some left, pick the meat out of the shells before it goes in the fridge — it keeps one day, and no longer. Bring it back in a frying pan over medium heat with 2 tbsp of water, tossing for 2 minutes. Never in a microwave, which cooks the shellfish a second time.
