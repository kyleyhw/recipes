## Ingredients

- 450 g spaghetti
- 680 g clams in the shell
- 450 g mussels in the shell
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 1 shallot, thinly sliced
- 8 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 1 tsp chilli flakes
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 180 ml dry white wine
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 4 tbsp flat-leaf parsley (a small bunch), chopped
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Method

1. Put the clams and mussels in a large bowl of cold, lightly salted water and leave them for 10 minutes — they spit out their grit into it. Drain them well, scrub the shells gently, and pull the beard from any mussel that still has one by gripping it and yanking towards the hinge.
2. Bring a large pan of salted water to a rolling boil for the spaghetti, and leave it boiling.
3. While it heats, put the olive oil in a large pot over medium-high heat for 2 minutes, until it shimmers and runs thin across the base.
4. Add the shallot and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until it has softened and gone translucent.
5. Add the garlic, half the chilli flakes and the salt. Cook for 1 minute, until the garlic smells sweet and the slices are gold at the rim only. Take it no further — browned garlic turns the whole sauce bitter, and there is no fixing it.
6. Pour in the wine and bring it to a boil. Add the clams and mussels, and cover the pot.
7. Drop the spaghetti into the boiling water at the same moment, and cook it for 2 minutes less than the packet says — it finishes in the sauce. Reserve 250 ml of the water, then drain.
8. Cook the shellfish for 5 to 7 minutes, shaking the pan occasionally. Lift each shell out with tongs into a large bowl as it opens, so nothing overcooks waiting for the rest. Throw out any that is still shut at the end.
9. Simmer the liquid left in the pot hard for 3 minutes, until it has reduced by half. This is the sauce, and reducing it is where its flavour comes from.
10. Add the drained spaghetti, the butter and 60 ml of the pasta water. Toss until the pasta is al dente and the sauce has thickened enough to cling to the strands rather than pool under them, adding more pasta water a tablespoon at a time — you may not need it all.
11. Toss the parsley through. Divide between bowls, top with the clams and mussels, scatter over the rest of the chilli flakes, and pass the lemon wedges at the table.

## Notes

Use a wine you would drink. Half of it boils away and the rest is the sauce, so a thin, sweet cooking wine makes a thin, sweet sauce.

Lifting the shells out as they open, rather than at the end, is worth the fiddling. Clams and mussels do not open at the same moment even within their own kind, and the ones that opened at four minutes are rubber by seven.

Do not salt the pot beyond the quarter teaspoon in step 5. The shellfish release their own liquor into it and it is seawater, and step 9 halves that liquor and doubles its salt.

## Storage

Eat it immediately. Shellfish reheated is shellfish made rubbery, and the sauce breaks on standing.

If there is some left, pick the meat out of the shells before it goes in the fridge — it keeps one day, and no longer. Bring it back in a frying pan over medium heat with 2 tbsp of water, tossing for 2 minutes. Never in a microwave, which cooks the shellfish a second time.
