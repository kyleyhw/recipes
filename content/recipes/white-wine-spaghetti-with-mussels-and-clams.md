## Ingredients

- 450 g spaghetti
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 1 shallot, thinly sliced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp chilli flakes
- 0.25 tsp salt
- 180 ml dry white wine
- 680 g clams in the shell
- 450 g mussels in the shell
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 lemon, zested and juiced
- 4 tbsp flat-leaf parsley (a small bunch), finely chopped
- 0.25 tsp black pepper, coarsely ground
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Method

1. Put the clams and mussels in a large bowl of cold, lightly salted water and leave them for 10 minutes — the salt draws the sand out of them. Drain them well, scrub the shells gently, and pull the beard from any mussel that still has one by gripping it and yanking towards the hinge.
2. Bring a large, heavy-bottomed pot of heavily salted water to a boil. While it heats, slice the shallot thinly, mince the garlic, grate the zest from one lemon and squeeze its juice, cut the second lemon into wedges, and finely chop the parsley.
3. Cook the spaghetti in the boiling water for 8 minutes, or 2 minutes less than the packet says — 2 minutes short of al dente, because it finishes in the sauce. Reserve 250 ml of the cooking water, drain the pasta, and dry the pot. The sauce is built in the same one.
4. Put the olive oil in the dry pot over medium-high heat until it shimmers and runs thin across the base. Add the shallot and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until it has softened.
5. Add the garlic, half the chilli flakes and the salt. Cook for 1 minute, until the garlic smells sweet. Do not let it brown — browned garlic turns the whole sauce bitter, and there is no fixing it.
6. Pour in the wine and bring it to a boil. Add the mussels and clams, cover, and cook for 5 to 7 minutes, shaking the pot occasionally. Lift each shell out with tongs into a large bowl as it opens, rather than waiting for the last of them. Throw out any that is still shut at the end.
7. Simmer the liquid left in the pot hard for 3 minutes, until it has reduced by half. Cut the butter into 3 pieces while it goes.
8. Add the drained spaghetti, the butter and 60 ml of the pasta water. Toss for 2 minutes, until the pasta is al dente and the sauce has thickened enough to cling to the strands rather than pool under them, adding more pasta water a tablespoon at a time — you may not need it all.
9. Add the lemon zest, the lemon juice, the parsley and the black pepper, and toss gently until combined. Taste, and season with more salt and pepper.
10. Take the pot off the heat. Return the mussels and clams and every drop of juice they have given up in the bowl, and toss. Divide between bowls, sharing the shellfish out evenly, scatter over the rest of the chilli flakes, and serve with the lemon wedges.

## Notes

Buy shellfish with tightly shut shells that smell of the sea and of nothing else. Littleneck clams are the ones this is written for. Clams cost more than mussels, so if the price is the problem, double the mussels and leave the clams out — the dish still works.

Keep them in something that breathes until you cook them: a bowl in the fridge under a damp cloth, never a sealed box or a bag of water. They are alive, and both of those suffocate them.

The source allows fettuccine or linguine in place of the spaghetti, at the same weight. Anything long — the sauce is thin enough that a short shape leaves it in the bottom of the bowl.

Use a crisp, dry, slightly fruity white with some acid in it — a Pinot Grigio or a Sauvignon Blanc. Half of it boils away and the rest is the sauce, so a thin, sweet cooking wine makes a thin, sweet sauce.

Lifting the shells out as they open, rather than all at once at the end, is worth the fiddling. They do not open at the same moment even within their own kind, and the ones that opened at four minutes are rubber by seven.

## Storage

Eat it immediately. Shellfish reheated is shellfish made rubbery, and the sauce breaks on standing.

If there is some left, pick the meat out of the shells before it goes in the fridge — it keeps one day, and no longer. Bring it back in a frying pan over medium heat with 2 tbsp of water, tossing for 2 minutes. Never in a microwave, which cooks the shellfish a second time.
