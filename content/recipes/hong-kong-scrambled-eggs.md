## Ingredients

- 4 eggs
- 60 ml evaporated milk
- 1 tsp cornflour
- 0.5 tsp salt
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 20 g unsalted butter

## Method

1. Stir the cornflour into the evaporated milk with a fork until no grains rasp on the bottom of the cup.
2. Beat the eggs in a bowl for 30 seconds, cutting through them rather than whipping, until the yolks and whites are one even yellow. Beat in the milk, the salt and the white pepper. Stop before it foams — air here becomes bubbles you can see in the finished eggs.
3. Pour the mixture through a fine sieve into a jug. What stays behind is the stringy chalaza, and it is the only lump the dish is capable of producing.
4. Melt the butter in a 20 cm non-stick pan over the lowest heat your hob will hold. It should melt without hissing, foaming or colouring; if it sizzles, the pan is already too hot, so take it off and let it cool.
5. Pour in the eggs and leave them completely alone for 20 seconds, until a thin skin has set on the base.
6. Push the spatula slowly across the base and lift, folding the set part over onto itself in wide sheets. Do this every 10 seconds or so for about 90 seconds. Do not stir, do not chop, and do not chase small pieces — you are building soft folds, not curds.
7. Take the pan off the heat while roughly a third of what is in it still looks like raw egg. This is the whole recipe and it will feel wrong.
8. Keep folding off the heat for 20 seconds. The pan's own heat sets the rest, and the eggs come to a stop glossy, barely holding their shape, and creamy the whole way through with no dry patches and no water in the pan.
9. Slide them onto hot buttered toast at once and eat them immediately.

## Notes

Off the heat while it looks underdone. Eggs carry enough heat in the pan to
finish themselves, and everyone who has made these badly has made them badly for
the same reason — they waited until the pan looked right, by which time the
plate is already too late.

Evaporated milk rather than cream or fresh milk. It brings more protein and more
solids and less water than milk, so the eggs stay tender without going watery
when they set; cream makes them richer but heavier, and this is meant to be
light enough to eat on toast at eight in the morning.

The teaspoon of cornflour is insurance. It gets between the egg proteins as they
tighten and buys perhaps twenty seconds of grace between perfect and overcooked,
which on a domestic hob is the difference between doing this well and doing it
well sometimes. Leave it out once you can hit the timing without it.

Low heat, and lower than you think. If the butter browns, the pan will scramble
the eggs into curds before the folding does anything, and there is no recovering
from that.

Four eggs in a 20 cm pan. Doubling it wants two pans rather than a bigger one:
in a wide pan the layer is thin and sets before you can fold it, and in a deep
heap the bottom overcooks while the top is still raw.

This is a cha chaan teng breakfast, and it is normally eaten with thick buttered
toast and a cup of Hong Kong Milk Tea, which is the other thing on this site
that the tin of evaporated milk is for.

## Storage

None. This is the rare dish that genuinely has to be eaten within a minute of
leaving the pan — it carries on setting on the plate, and eggs held warm for
five minutes are the eggs you were trying not to make.

Do not refrigerate and do not reheat. Cold set scrambled egg reheats to rubber
and weeps water, and there is no method that avoids it.
