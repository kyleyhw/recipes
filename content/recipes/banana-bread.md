## Ingredients

- 115 g unsalted butter
- 4 bananas, black-skinned and soft
- 200 g brown sugar
- 2 eggs
- 250 g all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1 tsp ground cardamom
- 80 g walnuts (a small handful), roughly chopped

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 175 °C. Line a 900 g loaf tin with baking paper, leaving the paper standing 2 cm above the rim.
2. Melt the butter in a light-coloured saucepan over medium heat. Keep it there for 4 to 6 minutes, swirling the pan, until the foam subsides and brown flecks settle on the base. It will smell of toffee. Pour it into a large bowl, scraping in every fleck, and leave for 10 minutes.
3. Mash the bananas on a plate with a fork until no lumps larger than a pea remain. Do not blend them: a purée makes the loaf gluey.
4. Whisk the brown sugar into the cooled butter until it looks like wet sand. Whisk in the eggs one at a time, 20 seconds each, then the mashed bananas.
5. In a second bowl, stir the flour, bicarbonate of soda, salt, cinnamon and cardamom together for 10 seconds, so the raising agent is evenly distributed before it meets any liquid.
6. Tip the dry ingredients into the wet. Fold with a spatula until the last streak of flour disappears, no more than 15 turns. Stop while the batter still looks rough.
7. Fold in the walnuts with 3 more turns. Scrape into the tin and level the top.
8. Bake for 55 to 65 minutes. It is done when a skewer pushed into the centre comes out with moist crumbs but no wet batter, and the top has split along its length.
9. Cool in the tin for 15 minutes, then lift it out by the paper onto a rack. Cool for a further hour before slicing, or it will tear.

## Notes

The bananas must be black, not merely spotted. Yellow bananas make a loaf that tastes of flour.

## Storage

Keeps four days at room temperature, wrapped in foil rather than cling film — foil keeps the crust from going tacky.

Freezes well cut into slices: wrap them individually, and they keep three months. Toast from frozen for 3 minutes, or microwave a slice for 20 seconds.

To bring a day-old loaf back, warm slices in a dry frying pan over medium heat for 90 seconds a side, until the cut faces are crisp. Do not microwave a whole loaf — it steams the crust soft.
