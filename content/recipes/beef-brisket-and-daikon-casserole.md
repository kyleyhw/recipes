## Ingredients

- 800 g beef brisket, in one piece
- 2000 ml water
- 40 g ginger, in 5 mm slices
- 30 g spring onions, whites only, bruised
- 2 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 3 g dried tangerine peel
- 2 star anise
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 2 bay leaves
- 700 g daikon, peeled and in 3 cm chunks
- 15 g rock sugar
- 2 tsp salt
- 1 tsp white pepper
- 10 g coriander, roughly chopped

## Method

1. Soak the tangerine peel in warm water for 20 minutes, then scrape the white pith off the inside with a teaspoon until only the thin dark outer layer is left. The pith is where the bitterness is, and leaving it on is what turns a clear broth sour by the second hour.
2. Cut the brisket into 4 cm pieces across the grain. Put them in a 24 cm saucepan, cover with cold water by 3 cm, and bring to a boil over high heat. Boil for 5 minutes: a grey-brown scum rises and sets in a raft on the surface.
3. Tip the whole panful into a colander and rinse each piece under the cold tap, rubbing the scum off with your fingers. Wash the pan out. This is the step that decides whether the finished broth is clear or cloudy, and there is no way to recover it later.
4. Return the meat to the clean pan with the 2000 ml of water, the ginger, spring onion whites, shaoxing wine, tangerine peel, star anise, cinnamon and bay leaves. Bring to a boil over high heat and skim off whatever rises for the first 3 minutes.
5. Drop the heat until the surface moves but never breaks — one bubble every second or two, no rolling. Set the lid ajar and simmer for 2 hours. A hard simmer emulsifies the fat into the liquid and turns it milky; this dish is judged on being able to see the bottom of the bowl.
6. Peel the daikon thickly, taking off the fibrous layer under the skin as well, and cut it into 3 cm chunks.
7. Add the daikon and simmer 30 minutes more, uncovered. It is done when it has gone from opaque white to translucent and a skewer goes through with no resistance at the centre. Added at the start it would be pulp by now, and this is the whole reason for the two stages.
8. Fish out the ginger, spring onion whites, star anise, cinnamon, bay and tangerine peel. Stir in the rock sugar, salt and white pepper, and simmer 2 minutes more until the sugar has gone.
9. Take the pan off the heat, cover it, and leave it 10 minutes. The daikon keeps drinking the broth while it stands and this is not a step to skip.
10. Serve in bowls with the coriander scattered over, and steamed rice alongside. The brisket should give under a spoon without needing a knife.

## Notes

This is Bao House's 萝卜牛腩砂锅 as far as anyone outside their kitchen can
write it. What is on the record is the menu — Beef Brisket w/ Daikon Soup
Casserole, across their Toronto shops — and what people say about eating it,
which is that it is light and refreshing. That last part is the load-bearing
detail, because it settles which of two dishes this is. Nobody has published
their recipe and this is not a transcription of it: it is the standard
Cantonese clear-broth version, written to be the thing their menu and their
customers describe.

The two dishes are worth telling apart before you shop. This one, 清湯蘿蔔牛腩,
is a clear soup — brisket, radish, aromatics, water, and nothing to colour or
thicken it. The other, 柱侯蘿蔔牛腩, is braised in chu hou paste and comes out
dark, glossy and thick, more a stew than a soup. They share their two main
ingredients and almost nothing else, and a recipe that starts by frying paste
in oil is the second one.

The brisket cut matters more than the grade. What a Cantonese kitchen wants is
坑腩 or 爽腩 — the fatty, sinewy end with the connective tissue running through
it, which is exactly the part a British butcher will sell you cheapest as plain
brisket. Lean brisket makes a thin, stringy pot. The sinew is not a defect to
trim off; two and a half hours turns it to gelatine and that gelatine is the
body of the broth.

Blanch and rinse, and do not skip the rinsing. Boiling for five minutes and
pouring the water away gets most of the scum, but a film of it stays stuck to
the meat, and rubbing each piece under the tap is what takes it off. A cloudy
broth cannot be clarified afterwards by any means available in a home kitchen.

There is no browning step and there should not be one. Searing the beef first
is the reflex from Western stews and it works against this dish: it darkens the
liquid, and the roasted flavour it adds is precisely what a clear broth is
avoiding.

A sand pot — 砂鍋, the unglazed clay casserole the menu name refers to — holds
heat well and is the traditional vessel, and it changes nothing about the
cooking. Any heavy pan does the same job. If you have one, warm it slowly and
never put it empty on a hot ring.

Two hours is the floor and it is not a target: brisket is done when it is done,
and a tough piece takes three. Test it before the daikon goes in, because
overcooked daikon cannot be undone and undercooked beef can simply carry on.

## Storage

Four days in the fridge and it is genuinely better on the second day — the
daikon spends the night drinking broth it was too crowded to take in the pan.
Reheat it gently on the hob, 10 minutes at a bare simmer, and never at a boil,
which cloudies a broth that has already been made clear once.

The fat sets into a firm cap in the fridge, which is the easiest time to decide
how much of it you want. Lift the whole disc off for a leaner bowl, or stir it
back in.

Freeze the beef and broth without the daikon, for three months. Daikon that has
been frozen thaws spongy and weeping and ruins the texture of everything around
it, so cook a fresh piece into the thawed broth for 30 minutes instead. This is
the one part of the dish that does not survive a freezer.
