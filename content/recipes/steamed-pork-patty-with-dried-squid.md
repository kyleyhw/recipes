## Ingredients

- 25 g dried squid
- 3 tbsp water
- 1 tsp cornflour
- 1 tbsp shaoxing wine
- 1 tsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 0.5 tsp sesame oil
- 250 g fatty pork mince
- 15 g ginger, finely minced
- 15 g spring onions, green parts only

## Method

1. Cover the dried squid with cold water and leave it 2 hours, or overnight in the fridge. It roughly triples in weight and turns from board-stiff to pliable. Warm water is faster and makes it rubbery, so start it before you do anything else.
2. Drain the squid, squeeze it, and cut it into slivers 3 mm wide and 2 cm long. Keep 2 tbsp of the soaking water and throw the rest away.
3. Put the reserved soaking water, the 3 tbsp of fresh water, the cornflour, shaoxing wine, soy sauce, sugar, white pepper and sesame oil in a mixing bowl and whisk for 20 seconds, until no cornflour is left on the bottom.
4. Add the pork, the ginger and the squid. Stir hard with chopsticks in one direction only for 60 to 90 seconds, and stop the moment it clings to the chopsticks in one mass.
5. Put a steaming rack in a wok or a wide pan, add water to just below it, and bring it to a rolling boil.
6. Spread the meat into a shallow heatproof dish in an even layer 1.5 cm thick, going right to the edges. Do not press it down.
7. Set the dish on the rack, cover with a tight lid, and steam over high heat for 12 minutes. It is done when the patty has shrunk from the sides of the dish, clear broth has gathered around it, and a knife pushed into the middle comes out with no pink on it.
8. Scatter the spring onion over the top and take it to the table in the dish it steamed in, with plain rice. Spoon the broth over the rice — it is the best broth of any of these patties.

## Notes

The two tablespoons of soaking water are not a garnish of an instruction. Most
of what came out of the squid in two hours is in it, and pouring the whole lot
away is throwing out the reason for soaking anything.

Dried squid is chewy on purpose. If yours is tough rather than chewy, it was
soaked in warm water or not for long enough — there is no fixing it at the
steaming stage.

This is the one version with soy sauce in it. The squid is deeply savoury but
not salty in the way zha cai or salted fish are, so the patty needs seasoning of
its own.

The technique is the same in every steamed patty here and is written out at
length in Steamed Pork Patty with Preserved Bean Curd: stir until it clings and
no further, 1.5 cm thick, 12 minutes over a rolling boil.

A chopped water chestnut belongs in this one more than in any of the others —
the squid is chewy and the chestnut is crisp, and the two textures against the
soft meat is the whole idea.

The panel puts the cholesterol high, and that is the squid rather than an error:
dried squid is one of the densest sources of it in any kitchen.

Optional, over the rice: Lee Kum Kee's seasoned soy sauce for seafood, or Roast
Meat Soy Dressing if there is no bottle.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, in the dish, covered, with its broth. Steam it again for
5 minutes, or microwave it covered for 90 seconds.

The raw mixture freezes for a month with the squid already in it, which is one
of the few times freezing improves something: the ice damages the squid's fibres
and it comes out slightly more tender than it went in.
