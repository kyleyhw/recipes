## Ingredients

- 2 g wakame
- 400 ml dashi
- 150 g silken tofu, in 1.5 cm cubes
- 2 tbsp miso
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Put the wakame in a small bowl of cold water. It swells to about ten times its size in 5 minutes; drain it and squeeze it gently.
2. Heat the dashi in a small pan until it steams and the first bubbles show at the edge. Do not let it boil.
3. Slide the tofu in and leave it 2 minutes, moving the pan rather than stirring, until the cubes are heated through. They break if you stir them and they are meant to arrive whole.
4. Take the pan off the heat. This is the step the dish stands on.
5. Put the miso in a ladle, hold it in the hot dashi so the liquid comes halfway up, and whisk it with chopsticks until it has dissolved and the ladle holds only thin brown liquid. Tip it into the pan and swirl once.
6. Add the wakame, ladle into two bowls, scatter the spring onion over and take it to the table. It is at its best in the next two minutes.

## Notes

**Never boil it after the miso goes in.** Miso is a live fermented paste and its
aroma is volatile: thirty seconds at a boil takes out the smell that is most of
what you are drinking and leaves a flat, salty, slightly gritty soup. Off the
heat, always, and reheat leftovers to steaming and no further.

Dissolve it in a ladle rather than dropping it in the pan. Miso dropped in whole
sinks, sticks and then needs stirring, which breaks the tofu and still leaves
lumps. The ladle takes ten seconds and is what every Japanese kitchen does.

Two tablespoons is 36 g and carries about 1300 mg of sodium between two bowls,
which is most of what the panel reports. That is what miso soup is; a weaker
soup is a reasonable thing to make and it will taste like weak miso soup rather
than like a lighter version of it.

The dashi is the whole flavour. There is nothing else in here doing any work,
so a soup made with instant granules tastes of instant granules and one made
with a proper packet tastes of the packet. This is the dish where the difference
is most obvious, which makes it a good one to compare two brands on.

What goes in it is not fixed and this combination is only the commonest one.
Wakame and tofu, spring onion and tofu, aburaage and greens, a handful of
chopped choy sum, leftover cooked vegetables, an egg cracked in and left to set
in the hot dashi for two minutes. The rule is that whatever it is should be
cooked before the miso goes in.

## Storage

A day in the fridge, and it is a compromise rather than a plan. The wakame goes
slippery and the tofu weeps. Reheat to steaming — not boiling — and accept that
it is second-day soup.

Better: keep the dashi and the miso apart. Brewed dashi keeps three days in the
fridge and freezes in an ice cube tray, and the soup is then four minutes from
cold whenever you want it.

It does not freeze once assembled: silken tofu comes out of a freezer as sponge.
