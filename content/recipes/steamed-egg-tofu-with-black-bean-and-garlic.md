## Ingredients

- 1 tbsp fermented black beans
- 15 g garlic, finely chopped
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 250 g egg tofu (1 tube)
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 0.25 tsp white pepper
- 2 tbsp water
- 15 g spring onions, finely sliced

## Method

1. Tip the black beans into a sieve and rinse them under cold water for 10 seconds, rubbing them between your fingers. They are packed in salt and the rinse takes the outside of it off — skip this and the dish is inedible.
2. Put them on a board and mash them with the flat of a knife until about half are crushed and half are whole. Whole beans give you salt bombs; a paste loses the texture. Half and half is the answer.
3. Heat the oil in a small pan over medium heat. Add the garlic and the mashed beans and fry for 60 seconds, stirring, until the garlic is pale gold and the smell turns from sharp to deep and savoury. This is blooming and it is what the oil is for — raw black beans steamed on top taste flat and slightly muddy.
4. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the soy sauce, sugar, white pepper and water.
5. Slit the tofu tube down its length and slide the tofu out in one piece. Cut it into rounds 2 cm thick and lay them overlapping along a heatproof plate with a rim.
6. Spoon the black bean mixture over the rounds, getting some onto each.
7. Put a rack in a wok or wide pan, add water to just below it, and bring it to a boil, then turn it down to a steady simmer. Set the plate on the rack, cover, and steam for 8 minutes. It is done when the tofu is hot all through and the sauce has thinned and run down between the rounds.
8. Scatter the spring onion over and serve at once, with rice, spooning the sauce from the plate over the rice.

## Notes

Rinse the beans, then bloom them in oil. Those two steps are the whole
difference between this and the disappointing version, and both get skipped
because neither looks important. Douchi are preserved in salt and coated in it;
frying them in oil with garlic is what converts a salty ferment into the smell
you actually want.

Half mashed, half whole. It sounds fussy and it is the texture of the dish.

A steady simmer, not a rolling boil — egg tofu is already set, and hard steam
pits it and makes it weep. Eight minutes is only there to heat it through and
let the sauce run.

No salt anywhere, and none needed. A tablespoon of douchi plus a tablespoon of
soy is already about 1100 mg of sodium across two servings, which is why the
teaspoon of sugar is in there.

This is the same seasoning as black bean spare ribs and steamed fish with black
beans, and once you have made it once you have all three. Spread it over 300 g
of pork ribs and steam for 25 minutes instead.

## Storage

Two days in the fridge, covered, on the plate. It reheats better than the fried
egg tofu dishes because there is no crust to lose — steam it again for 4 minutes
or microwave it covered for 90 seconds.

It does not freeze: the tofu thaws spongy.
