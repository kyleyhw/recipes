## Ingredients

- 25 g bread flour, for the tangzhong
- 125 ml milk, for the tangzhong
- 275 g bread flour
- 40 g caster sugar
- 6 g salt
- 5 g fast-action dried yeast
- 130 ml milk, warmed to 35 °C
- 30 g unsalted butter, softened

## Method

1. Whisk the 25 g of bread flour into the 125 ml of milk in a small saucepan until smooth, then cook over medium heat, stirring constantly with a spatula, for 3 minutes. It thins, then thickens all at once at about 65 °C into a paste that holds the lines the spatula draws in it. Scrape it into a bowl, press cling film onto the surface, and leave it 20 minutes until it is no warmer than your hand.
2. Stir the 275 g of bread flour, the sugar, the salt and the yeast together in a large bowl, keeping the salt and the yeast on opposite sides until they are through the flour.
3. Add the warm milk and the cooled tangzhong and mix to a shaggy dough. Knead on an unfloured surface for 12 minutes. It is very sticky for the first five and then turns smooth and slack; scrape rather than flour the worktop.
4. Work in the butter a third at a time, 5 minutes in all. Knead until a piece stretched between your fingers goes thin enough to read through without tearing. This dough will do that better than any other in the collection, which is what the tangzhong bought you.
5. Cover the bowl and prove for 60 minutes at room temperature, until doubled and a floured finger pushed 2 cm in leaves a dent that fills back only halfway.
6. Knock it back, divide into 3 pieces of 200 g, and roll each into a ball. Rest them 10 minutes under a cloth or they will fight the rolling pin.
7. Roll one ball into a 20 cm oval, fold the two long sides into the middle to make a strip 8 cm wide, then roll it up tightly from the short end into a swiss roll. Repeat with the other two. Set the three rolls into a lined 900 g loaf tin, spirals facing the same way, with gaps between them.
8. Prove for 60 minutes, until the rolls have joined into one loaf and the highest point is 1 cm above the rim of the tin. Under-proved, it tears at the side in the oven; over-proved, it collapses when it goes in.
9. Heat the oven to 180 °C and bake for 30 minutes. It is done when the top is deep brown, the loaf sounds hollow tapped underneath, and it has come away from the sides of the tin.
10. Turn it out onto a rack immediately — left in the tin it steams and the sides go damp — and leave it 60 minutes before cutting. Cutting a hot enriched loaf drags the crumb into a paste.

## Notes

The tangzhong is 5% of the flour cooked with five times its weight in milk, and
it is the only reason this loaf is different from any other enriched bread.
Cooked starch holds several times the water raw starch will, so the finished
dough carries far more water than it could otherwise take without becoming
unworkable, and that water is what keeps the crumb soft on day three. It is a
Japanese formalisation of the same idea as an old Chinese water roux, and it
costs five minutes.

Bread flour throughout, including in the tangzhong. Plain flour makes a loaf
that will not climb the tin and tears at the shoulders.

Three rolls rather than one lump. Rolling and coiling each piece lines the
gluten up in one direction, which is what gives shokupan its strands and lets
it tear along the grain rather than crumbing.

The lid is the other decision. Baked open as written, it rises into a domed
loaf; a pullman tin with the lid on gives the flat-topped square brick sold in
Japan, at 180 °C for 30 minutes the same. Neither is more correct.

This is what Kaya Toast and Hong Kong French Toast mean by white bread, and
both are noticeably better made with it — which is the reason this recipe is
here at all, since the collection has been calling for a loaf it could not
make.

## Storage

Three days at room temperature in a bag, cut side down on a board, and the
tangzhong is what buys the third day; an ordinary white loaf is stale by the
second.

Never the fridge. Bread stales fastest just above freezing — several times
faster than at room temperature — so a loaf in the fridge is worse on day two
than one left out.

Freeze it sliced, in a bag with the air pressed out, for three months. It
toasts straight from frozen with no thawing and comes out indistinguishable
from fresh, which is what makes slicing it before freezing worth the two
minutes.
