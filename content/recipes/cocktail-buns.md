## Ingredients

- 300 g bread flour
- 40 g caster sugar
- 5 g salt
- 7 g fast-action dried yeast
- 150 ml milk, warmed to 35 °C
- 1 egg
- 40 g unsalted butter, softened
- 50 g unsalted butter, softened, for the filling
- 50 g icing sugar
- 60 g desiccated coconut
- 40 g white bread, crusts on, blitzed to crumbs
- 1 egg yolk, for the filling
- 20 g unsalted butter, softened, for the stripe
- 20 g icing sugar, for the stripe
- 25 g all-purpose flour
- 1 egg yolk, for the glaze
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds

## Method

1. Stir the bread flour, caster sugar, salt and yeast together in a large bowl for 10 seconds, keeping the salt and the yeast apart until they are through the flour.
2. Pour in the milk and the whole egg, mix to a shaggy dough, and knead on an unfloured surface for 10 minutes, until it is smooth and elastic and no longer sticks to your hands. Do not add flour.
3. Work the 40 g of butter in a third at a time, 4 minutes in all, until a piece of dough stretches thin enough to see light through without tearing.
4. Cover and prove for 75 minutes, until doubled and a floured finger pushed 2 cm in leaves a dent that fills back only halfway.
5. While it proves, beat the 50 g of butter with the 50 g of icing sugar for 2 minutes until pale, then work in the coconut, the breadcrumbs and the first egg yolk with a spatula. It comes together as a firm paste that holds a ball when squeezed. Chill it for 20 minutes and divide it into 8 pieces of 25 g, rolled into logs 8 cm long.
6. For the stripe, beat the 20 g of butter with the 20 g of icing sugar, work in the all-purpose flour and 2 tsp of water, and put it in a small piping bag with a 3 mm plain nozzle. It should be soft enough to pipe and stiff enough to hold a line.
7. Knock the dough back and divide it into 8 pieces of 65 g. Roll each into a 12 cm oval, lay a log of filling down the middle, and fold the long sides over it, pinching the seam and the ends shut. Any gap leaks butter onto the tray and the bun collapses into it.
8. Set them seam down on two lined trays, 6 cm apart, and prove for 45 minutes, until half again as big and slow to spring back from a light press.
9. Heat the oven to 190 °C. Beat the second egg yolk with 1 tsp of water and brush the buns. Pipe a single line of the stripe paste down the length of each, then scatter the sesame seeds over.
10. Bake for 15 minutes, swapping the shelves at 8 minutes, until deep golden brown all over and the stripe has set matt and pale against it.
11. Cool on a rack for 20 minutes. The filling is molten straight out of the oven.

## Notes

The breadcrumbs are the whole history of this bun. It is a Hong Kong bakery's
answer to the offcuts and unsold loaves piling up at the end of a day: blitz
them, bind them with butter, sugar and coconut, and sell them back inside a
fresh bun. Leaving them out and adding more coconut makes a filling that is
sandy and weeps butter — the crumb is what holds it together.

Stale bread works better than fresh, which is convenient. Fresh crumbs are wet
and clump; a slice left out overnight blitzes to something drier and looser.
Panko is not the substitute it looks like, being much coarser.

Unsweetened desiccated coconut, not the sweetened flakes for decorating. There
are already 50 g of icing sugar in a 200 g filling.

The name has nothing to do with drinks. 雞尾包 means cocktail in the sense of a
mixture of leftovers, which is exactly what it is, and the English name is a
straight translation of a joke.

The dough is the Pineapple Buns dough, unchanged, through step 4. Make a
double batch and split it, and you have both for one kneading — which is how a
bakery does it, and why the two turn up in the same window.

## Storage

Two days at room temperature in a paper bag. The filling keeps the crumb around
it soft for longer than a plain bun, so day two is better here than it is for a
pineapple bun.

Reheat at 160 °C for 6 minutes. Take them out and wait 5 minutes before biting
one — the sugar and butter in the middle get much hotter than the bread and
hold it.

They freeze baked for a month, bagged with the air pressed out, and reheat from
frozen at 170 °C for 12 minutes. The filling freezes better than the dough
does, so this is a good thing to have in a freezer.
