# Recipe photographs

Every recipe here can carry one picture, generated by an image model and
credited on the page as generated. The mechanics are in
[`docs/photos.md`](../../../docs/photos.md); this is what makes a *good* one.

These rules were written after looking at the first 47 photographs and finding
five repeatable faults. Each rule exists because something specific went wrong.

---

## 1. The model must be told what is in the dish

It cannot see the ingredient list unless you pass it. Left to the title and the
description it invents plausible contents.

> The banana bread came back with what read as chocolate chips. The recipe has
> walnuts.

Pass the parsed ingredient **names**, no quantities, and say the list is
exhaustive: *"It contains only: …"*. Quantities are noise to an image model and
push it toward drawing measuring spoons.

## 2. Take the finish from the diagram, not from the last step

The last method step is only sometimes a plating step. When it is about cooling
or resting it says nothing about how the dish looks.

> Banana bread's last step is "cool for a further hour before slicing". Mapo
> tofu's mentions sichuan pepper and rice but not the spring onions from step
> 10 — and the photograph has no spring onions on it.

The diagram already encodes this exactly: **the root operation's leaf children
are the things that go on at the end.** Use those. They are the garnish, by
construction, and they are right where prose is accidental.

## 3. Compose for the card, not for the hero

Stored images are 16:9. The recipe page's hero is `aspect-16/9` and fits them,
but a listing card is `aspect-4/3` — so **roughly an eighth is cropped off each
side of every card**, which is where most people see the picture first.

> Mapo tofu's bowl of rice, the soy dressing's saucepan and the tiramisu's sieve
> all sit exactly in the strip that gets cut.

Tell the model to keep the dish and anything else that matters inside the
central square, and that the outer eighth of each side may be lost.

**This is not fixed by changing the stored ratio while the collection is
half-generated.** Storing 4:3 would suit the card better and is worth doing on a
full regeneration; doing it piecemeal would leave a book with two shapes in it.
Until then, compose defensively.

## 4. Say when nothing is browned

Food models default to colour and char. A dish that is pale needs to say so.

> Ginger scallion oil came back with charred brown flecks in it. It is raw
> ginger and spring onion with hot oil poured over — nothing in it is cooked at
> all.

Look at whether the method contains any of *fry, sear, brown, roast, char,
grill, caramelise, toast*. Where it does not, say plainly that nothing in the
dish is browned or charred.

## 5. Frame by category, not generically

A sauce photographed as a plate of rice is a card that lies about what it is.

> The roast meat soy dressing is a condiment. Its picture is a plate of rice
> with dressing on it — accurate to its notes, and wrong for a card whose job is
> to say "this is a sauce".

- **Sauces & Condiments** — a small bowl of the thing itself, nothing else
- **Drinks** — one cup or glass
- **Baked Goods** — whole, with one slice or piece cut and set beside it
- **Soups & Stews** — one deep bowl
- **everything else** — one plate or one shallow bowl

An accompaniment (rice, bread) belongs in the frame only where a method step
actually names one.

## New photographs must match the 47 that already exist

This is the constraint that outranks every improvement below it. The collection
is generated in batches, and a batch that looks different from the last one is
worse than a batch with a fault in it — one is a book, the other is a pile.

So when changing the prompt, **change what is in the frame, never how it is
photographed.** The style sentences below are load-bearing and should move
verbatim from one version of the prompt to the next:

- slight overhead angle
- plain matte ceramic
- plain wood or stone surface
- soft daylight from one side
- as it would look cooked at home, not styled for a restaurant

Two consequences worth stating:

- **Vessel guidance changes the noun, not the material.** "A plain matte ceramic
  cup" for a drink, not "a glass tumbler". The category rule below picks what
  the food sits in; it must not pick a new look.
- **Framing instructions must not make shots wider.** "Keep anything essential
  out of the outer eighth" is easily read as "leave a margin", and a batch
  composed further back is a visible mismatch. Say *centred and filling the
  frame*, then say what must not be at the edges.

## The house style, which is working and should not be changed

Slight overhead angle. Plain matte ceramic. Plain wood or stone surface. Soft
daylight from one side. Photographed as it would look cooked at home, not styled
for a restaurant.

Banned outright: text, labels, watermarks, hands, people, decoratively arranged
cutlery, branded packaging, artificial steam, more than one dish, and any
ingredient not on the list passed in.

All 47 of the first batch share this and look like one book. That consistency is
the most valuable thing about them and is worth more than any individual
improvement.

## Reviewing a generated picture

Open it and ask, in this order:

1. **Is anything in it that is not in the ingredient list?**
2. **Is anything missing that the diagram says goes on at the end?**
3. **Would it survive being cropped to 4:3?** Cover the outer eighth of each
   side with your thumbs and see what is lost.
4. **Is anything browned that the method never browns?**
5. **Does it look like the category** — a sauce like a sauce, a drink like a
   drink?

A picture that fails 1 or 2 is wrong and should be regenerated. Failing 3, 4 or
5 is a judgement call at about seven pence an image.

---

## Filling gaps without redrawing the book

Editing the prompt changes every recipe's fingerprint at once, so the ordinary
staleness rule immediately declares the whole collection due. That is right when you mean to
regenerate and expensive when you do not.

    npm run photos -- --missing

draws only recipes that have no picture file at all and leaves everything else
alone, whatever the prompt now says. Use it for every batch until there is a
deliberate decision to redraw the whole collection.

The consequence to accept: for a while the book contains photographs made under
two versions of the prompt. That is the right trade — a missing picture is worse
than a slightly inconsistent one — but it is also why the style sentences must
not change between versions. Content rules may improve batch to batch; the look
may not.

## Cost and pacing

Regenerating is cheap enough not to agonise over — see `docs/photos.md` for
current prices and the spend ceiling — but **editing the prompt changes every
recipe's fingerprint and re-draws the whole book.** Test a change with
`--only <slug>` or `--limit 3` before running it across the collection, and
check `--dry-run` output first, which costs nothing.
